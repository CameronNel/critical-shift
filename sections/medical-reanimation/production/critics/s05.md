It also exposes the remaining shortcomings: rigid-looking fabric, unusually uniform and pristine surface response, and small props whose forms still look assembled rather than handled.

## Biggest observed gaps

1. **C00b, folded articles on the right shelf; C00, hanging towel:** fabric is still not convincingly established. The folded articles have planar tops, sharply regular perimeter edges and an even stacked profile that read as rigid pads. The hanging article has a much better silhouette than s03 but still appears firm and smooth. This remains a central slice failure because material identity is part of the style proof.
2. **C00 and C00b, major surfaces:** the workstation, wall, door, floor and props retain a near-new, very even finish. The approved references use restrained but visible variation and specific contact wear. The distinction is not missing photographic grunge; the current surfaces lack enough authored evidence of material and use at either reviewed distance.
3. **C00b, basin interior and rim transitions:** the basin is appreciably improved, but a conspicuous triangular/sliver-like form appears inside the left side of the bowl, and the transition around the rear working edge remains visually irregular. This needs an evaluated-geometry check; the image alone does not establish whether it is intended construction, an intersection or shading artifact. It is too prominent in this diagnostic view to ignore.
4. **C00b, small human props:** the brush bristles read as a rigid repeated comb, and the orange discarded item on the bin reads as separated tubular/flat pieces more readily than a limp, continuous glove. Their function is suggested, but their authored form and material quality lag behind the larger workstation.
5. **C00b, utility and basin-support detail:** some fittings and small terminal forms remain difficult to distinguish from exposed primitive ends or overlapping pieces. The cluster now has enough equipment; clarity and construction finish matter more than increasing the number of objects.

## Technical findings and limits

- Entry width is reported as the minimum of 18 horizontal ray probes: **2.2 m**. Entry height is reported as the minimum of 15 upward probes: **2.5 m**. The report explicitly states that distributed probes may miss protrusions between samples.
- Arrival and wash routes pass a continuous swept **0.6 m square by 1.8 m standing envelope** against evaluated world-space object bounds. These are useful pedestrian-clearance results, not a cart or ragdoll simulation.
- The validated navigation domain covers only the slice. The route starts at the inside entry approach; exterior access, complete cart turning/loading and ragdoll motion remain unverified.
- The report explicitly says a **2.0 m transverse cart** centered in the doorway leaves only **0.1 m per side**, preventing a 0.6 m person from passing beside it. Manual clearing is required and runtime clearing is **not verified**. This must remain an open requirement; it cannot be reported as dropped-cart robustness passed.
- Fourteen registered support contacts pass. The audit identifies **197 unclassified geometry objects** and sets `exhaustive_support_taxonomy_verified` to **false**. The headline `pass: true` applies only to the included passing checks.
- No final cold-start result or comprehensive evaluated-scene intersection result is supplied for this review.

The technical report is significantly more useful because it states these boundaries clearly. Acceptance still requires the missing evidence and correction of the visual gaps. No full production approval is granted.
