## Inherited envelope / orientation consistency — 94

The image preserves the open-front chamber, adult berth running horizontally across the machine, and the two separated pedestal supports. That is consistent with the inherited 4.30 m wall-length assembly and the berth long axis along the wall. The berth surface presents its transfer direction out through the open face, matching the existing R09 relationship.

This tight component crop has no wall datum, room corner, scale bar, or rear-service view. It cannot prove the exact 4.30 m × 2.50 m bounds, west placement, east bypass, rear clearance, portal passage, or southwest cart bay. Those remain scene checks and are not changed by the concept.

## Transfer / handling interface — 92

The segmented berth, orange retention straps, front rail, visible hinge hardware, and supported deck make a practical load surface readable. The open mouth and continuous-looking front rail preserve the M04 transfer relationship and avoid the earlier thin-pallet ambiguity.

The close-up does not show the separate wheeled cart, folding bridge in a deployed state, casters, or a loaded body. Per the targeted-hero scope, their absence from this component image is supplemental rather than a defect. The final scene must still prove the cart/bridge engagement and body transfer in its designated views and physical tests.

## Machinery construction / specificity — 96

M06 is materially more specific than the M03 render. The left receiver has a recessed face, a keyed-looking insert drawer, an orange control handle, a separate lower release control, fasteners, and a framed housing. The right suit-service station has an industrial locking collar, black dry-rubber hose, mounting clip, and a clear `SUIT` header. The berth has segmented pads, straps, a steel front hinge/rail assembly, two articulated support stacks, fasteners, and distinct side-wall panels. The chamber also gains folded jamb skins, deep panel breaks, gasket-like perimeter treatment, and practical vertical task lights.

These are concrete manufactured distinctions rather than repeated bevels, holograms, or generic hospital fixtures. M06 does not define interaction IDs, host ownership, power draw, cartridge persistence, or restart states; those remain interface/runtime responsibilities.

## Human-scale operation — 94

The berth reads adult-sized, with sensible working height, support spacing, strap reach, release placement, hose loop, coupling height, and service access. The central bed spans the chamber mouth without looking decorative. A human or cart dimension marker is not present, so exact reach clearance and collision behavior remain unverified.

## Label and control readability — 92

`INSERT`, `SUIT`, and `RELEASE` are short, high-contrast, unobstructed, and mounted behind their controls. This directly addresses the M03 failure where label stems obscured the functional words. The insert and release controls are visually separable, and the suit coupling is immediately associated with its label.

The crop does not visibly show an `OCRU` mark. That is a remaining canonical-signage check for the actual scene, rather than permission to invent a replacement expansion. The only authoritative long name remains **Organic Continuity and Recommissioning Unit (OCRU)**. M06’s visible labels should be carried over only where they match the interface vocabulary.

## Material family separation — 95

Pale painted manufactured skins, graphite/black interior panels, darker dry-rubber hose, brighter steel rail and fasteners, and restrained orange safety controls separate cleanly. The broad fields are quiet and game-readable, with localized edge wear on handled frame edges. The result avoids the universal satin-plastic response and the photoreal grunge that caused M01 to fail.

The generated image has a polished product-render finish. Blender translation should preserve the family separation and broad roughness differences while authoring its own restrained wear; exact generated surface marks are not evidence or requirements.

## Lighting / contact readability — 94

Two neutral task lights establish the chamber working zone, while the frame and control edges receive warmer highlights. The dark rear panels retain depth and the berth supports cast contact shadows onto the floor, making the assembly sit in the chamber. The lighting supports the functional labels and orange controls without a colored chamber glow.

This is a single close hero and does not establish the room’s complete practical-light hierarchy or every fixed-camera exposure. The scene should retain the neutral/no-teal intent while validating entry, reverse, decon, rear-service, and materials views.

## Strict Valorant / no-teal fidelity — 97

M06 follows the requested grounded Valorant-influenced language: bold readable silhouettes, clean graphic tonal fields, functional orange accents, specific industrial construction, authored negative space, and restrained wear. No teal or cyan appears in the surfaces, hose, controls, or lights. It avoids neon sci-fi treatment, decorative greebles, luxury hospital cues, horror styling, and generic concrete grunge.
