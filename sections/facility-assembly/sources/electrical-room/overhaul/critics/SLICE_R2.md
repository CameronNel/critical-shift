# Independent R2 style-slice review — repair bench bay

**Style gate decision: Fit to expand.** This is a pass of the validation slice as evidence that the room's visual language can be carried into the wider build. It is not whole-room art acceptance, and it does not waive the owner's separate requirement that every final electrical-room category exceed 98/100. Expansion should carry the construction, material and lighting gains visible here; full-room review still needs the mandatory views and its own independent score.

**Evidence inspected:** `renders/slice-R2/C09_Workbench.png` and `renders/slice-R2/C08_Material_Detail.png`, compared against the R1 images, the C09 baseline, and the calibrated reworked spawn-room reference. I judged the two fixed cameras together: C09 shows the full task lamp and its context; C08 is a close material view that intentionally crops the lamp. That crop is not a lighting defect when C09 supplies the source context.

## R1 target check

- **Test components:** Resolved. The stepped ceramic bodies, collars/caps and copper terminals in C08 have a readable electrical-component silhouette at close range and remain distinct in C09.
- **Gloves:** Resolved enough for the style gate. C08 shows a paired cluster with cuff openings and broad glove volume; C09 retains the PPE read at the wider framing.
- **Warm material separation:** Resolved. The grey tool board no longer merges chromatically with the brown phenolic top.
- **Wall construction:** Resolved enough for expansion. C09 shows panel returns and shadowed boundaries instead of only the long seam lines criticized in R1. Keep this restrained joint depth consistent when repeating the panel system.
- **Lighting hierarchy:** Resolved. C09 shows the full task light and cooler neutral fill; the more amber task pool is local rather than washing the whole bay.
- **Recent work evidence:** Resolved. C08/C09 show localized marks and handled equipment, while the canvas maintenance bag and paperwork support the scene's purpose without adding arbitrary debris.

The remaining opportunities—slightly more tactile breakup on the large wall fields and more irregular placement of one small item—are polish targets, not style blockers. The slice now demonstrates grounded stylized construction, distinct materials, readable practical lighting and purposeful dressing well enough to test those rules across the room. Keep the same disciplined density and quiet wall areas during expansion; do not copy every bench detail everywhere. No geometry, support-contact or other numerical technical claim is made by this visual review.
