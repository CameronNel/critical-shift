## R13-to-R14 changes and stability

R14 preserves R13's room composition, clear 2.8 m aisle, machine positions, restrained warm-concrete/green/charcoal/oxide palette, and practical-light hierarchy. The changes are localized to the sorter optical head, press access cover and small maintenance/work details. The process path remains easy to read from `CAM_ENTRY`, `CAM_MAIN_ROUTE`, `CAM_REVERSE` and `CAM_MINE_TO_CRUSHER`; the dark openings in `CAM_REVERSE` and `CAM_DISPATCH` remain adjacent-map interfaces, and the clean center floor remains deliberate circulation space. I found no new visible route blockage or composition regression. The broad visual read is stable across the R13→R14 correction cycle.

The two principal fabrication changes read clearly. In `CAM_PINCH`, the SO-04 sensor now has a dark inset window with a visibly slotted mount; its optic reads as a deliberate sensor assembly rather than the prior blank plate on a vertical bar. In `CAM_MATERIAL`, the press access door now has a stepped pressed perimeter, a clearer pull and visible fasteners. These details add construction logic without overwhelming the machinery silhouette. They also track the Spawn reference's believable hardware and layered prop construction while keeping the refinery's process-driven style.

The large process stations remain readable and visually consistent, and the hero tank retains its strong orange pressure-vessel silhouette, gauges, clamped access hatch, valves and curved external line. `CAM_HERO_DETAIL` confirms the PV-05 placard and pressure dial are separated and readable; there is no label/dial collision. The localized changes do not disrupt this focal area. Material response remains controlled and generally matte-to-semi-matte, with distinct enamel, painted steel, glass and structural metal. I see no generic glossy response or noisy surface treatment.

Practical lighting remains well judged. The validation report records zero world illumination and 21 interior AREA fixtures bound to visible lenses. The renders keep useful warm pools, falloff and shadowed secondary corners while retaining readability. `CAM_WORK_NOOK` continues to show the clearest human cluster through the shift board, radio, mug and food. The added glove and inspection/tool details are supported technically, but they do not form a strong new visual read from the fixed views: in `CAM_ASSEMBLY`, the glove on the worktop is too small or occluded by the slanted control panel and nearby apparatus to identify confidently. The wipe/caliper are likewise too subtle at this camera scale. That leaves the worker-story score below the rest of the candidate despite their purpose and support.

The actual Spawn views remain a stronger benchmark for readable use: the prep room groups clothing, bench objects, posted notes and physical hardware so each reads from the review angle. R14 has better process equipment construction than a generic kitbash and preserves useful quiet floor space; the remaining difference is the legibility and distribution of human work evidence, not the quantity of objects in the clear aisle.
