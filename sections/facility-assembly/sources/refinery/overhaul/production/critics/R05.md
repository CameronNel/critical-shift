# Independent correction-cycle review — R05

**Reviewer:** independent GPT-6 Luna critic

**Scope:** all 11 completed R05 fixed views, compared with R04 and evaluated against the refinery brief, Critical Shift art direction, owner rubric and Spawn quality-reference renders. The candidate is the additive standalone refinery module; preserved open map ports and practical-only illumination are part of the review.

**Decision:** **R05 improves route communication, architectural construction and technical evidence, but remains substantially below the ≥99-per-category and weighted ≥99 acceptance threshold.** Scores reflect R05 pixels and supplied source evidence only; future work receives no credit.

## R04-to-R05 change assessment

**Improvements confirmed in R05 pixels:**

- The entry and route views are calmer: the prominent foreground cart is removed and clear route markings plus the `KEEP CLEAR` stencil make the aisle legible (`CAM_ENTRY`, `CAM_MAIN_ROUTE`, `CAM_REVERSE`).
- Wall and threshold construction is more deliberate, with added vertical panels, supports and routed services (`CAM_REVERSE`, `CAM_MINE_TO_CRUSHER`).
- The assembly/inspection group has a more finished base and integrated storage rather than reading solely as open-legged tables (`CAM_ASSEMBLY`).
- The work board now has a coherent set of shift and process notes instead of R04's sparse cards (`CAM_WORK_NOOK`).
- More practical lighting is visible around the threshold and work area. Technical checks still tie every source to a physically present lens inside the room and keep world strength at zero.
- Ore in the receiving hopper strengthens the first step of the material chain (`CAM_MINE_TO_CRUSHER`).

**R04 defects that remain or appear:**

- The machine line still shares a repeated simple vocabulary: broad green controls, orange cabinet faces, similar supports and dark bases. The added architecture has more paneling, but this does not yet provide the distinct primary shapes needed for a high-level environmental art finish.
- The main control panel in `CAM_PINCH` remains prominent against the sorter/conveyor throat and obscures the equipment read.
- The open-port views still render as black gaps beyond the doorway returns, particularly `CAM_DISPATCH`; they preserve the module contract but weaken the standalone image's sense of connected space.
- The new upper/side gauge in `CAM_HERO_DETAIL` is too large relative to the tank's other gauges and reads as attached to a vent collar without a clear mounting bracket or functional cue.
- The painted aisle lines help route clarity but are visually strong and discontinuous in places, making them compete with the process line rather than consistently guiding the eye.
- Materials remain very clean and broad. New wall panels improve construction logic, but the surfaces have little differentiated roughness, handling wear or repair evidence.
- `CAM_ASSEMBLY` shows the inspection scanner assembly above the worktop. Independent measurement finds both scanner cheeks 22.5 mm clear of the work surface, with the bridge and task lamp carried by these unsupported roots; neither cheek has a support-registry entry despite the stored validator passing.

## Camera-by-camera defects

- **CAM_ENTRY:** clear stencil and cart removal improve the near-floor composition. The far equipment still reads as a row of simple machines, and the aisle markings occupy a large share of the foreground.
- **CAM_MAIN_ROUTE:** white floor lines distinguish circulation, but the black gridded drain and yellow/white route markings compete for attention. The main panel and repeated machine bases still dominate the process row.
- **CAM_PROCESS:** the new hatch adds useful focal specificity; the surrounding green drum and equipment cabinet remain smooth, box-like and less developed. Small console text is not legible at gameplay distance.
- **CAM_REVERSE:** threshold construction, wall services and route marks feel more intentional. Both connected openings remain almost black beyond their frames; the hanging safety/cleanup object is not immediately identifiable at this distance.
- **CAM_PINCH:** the large tilted operator panel still intrudes into the belt/sorter composition. Its oversized emergency buttons read clearly, but the panel and conveyor supports crowd the focal area.
- **CAM_MATERIAL:** glass is readable and controls have a hierarchy. The panel is still a broad face on thin supports, and the press/worktop surfaces show little contact wear or distinct roughness.
- **CAM_ASSEMBLY:** the green cabinet base and stocked shelf make the workstation feel more constructed and used. The large right-hand port is still nearly black and competes with the worktable; the station family remains mechanically generic.
- **CAM_DISPATCH:** added wall treatment and shelving improve the foreground, but the black open port takes up much of the frame. The cylinder cart is readable, although the destination/receiving area beyond the port is not.
- **CAM_MINE_TO_CRUSHER:** contained ore and better wall service routing add process context. The receiving hopper and diagonal conveyor still consume the left/center silhouette, while a large quiet wall and floor remain under-articulated.
- **CAM_WORK_NOOK:** the shift board is materially clearer and more purposeful. The mug, radio and food grouping remains small, with the rest of the frame dominated by blank wall; the posted text is too small for gameplay reading.
- **CAM_HERO_DETAIL:** the access hatch is the strongest authored detail in the round. The new gauge mounted at the side collar is conspicuously larger than its neighbors; the smooth vessel skin and cropped adjacent drum/pipe context still constrain the process story.

## Highest-impact remaining fixes

1. Redesign at least the crusher/sorter and dryer/inspection as distinct authored primary shapes within their existing floor bounds. Establish specific frames, guards, service doors, supports and input/output faces for each stage instead of varying only the front panel and color.
2. Move the pinch-point console onto a clear machine-side service face and reduce its width/depth so the conveyor crossing and material flow read as one unobstructed functional composition.
3. Close the measured scanner gap by lowering both cheek bases to the worktop or adding real support feet, then register both roots so validation tests them. Include this worktop-dependent cluster in the rebuilt support report.
4. Resolve practical scale and hierarchy around the hero: reduce or remount the side-collar gauge, make the vessel's hatch/gauges relate to distinct functions, and show clear pipe destinations. Preserve the pressure-vessel access hatch as the current strongest machine feature.
5. Refine route graphics into one consistent aisle language and retain clear floor between route and process strip. Keep the cart out of the entry route; reintroduce a small service cluster only where a specific machine needs it.
6. Improve the open-port read through physical interior jamb/lintel fixtures and readable inner returns. Keep the ports open, avoid exterior or invisible lights, and use the assembly view to show adjacent connected space when judging the final assembled map.
7. Add selected material response and use evidence: localized scuffs at the aisle and cart/service contacts, restrained enamel edge wear, broader roughness variation on concrete, and a clearly different response for rubber and bare metal. Keep broad quiet walls quiet rather than adding dense trim everywhere.
8. Expand worker evidence at a few process points with one meaningful handled record, spare part or tool placed on a supported work surface. The current board is a good foundation; let a few work details carry further into the broad views without filling every open area.
