## R03-to-R04 change assessment

**Improvements confirmed in R04 pixels:**

- The pressure-vessel blank disc is now a recognizable clamped access hatch with a central hub/lever. It makes the vessel feel more manufactured and serviceable (`CAM_PROCESS`, `CAM_HERO_DETAIL`).
- Control faces have a more deliberate operator hierarchy: larger emergency buttons and varied switch types are visible at `CAM_PINCH` and `CAM_MATERIAL`.
- Ore is visible in the receiving hopper and gives the process chain a more legible input state (`CAM_MINE_TO_CRUSHER`).
- Maintenance and safety cues are stronger: the cart carries a tool/rag cue, the wall service grouping is more articulated, and `CAM_ASSEMBLY` gains a stocked shelf.
- The work nook ledger is cleaner and more readable; its short “KEEP IT FLOWING” note provides a human cue without a large wall of text.
- The R02 floor-pegs error remains repaired across R04 views.

**R03 defects that remain or appear:**

- The cart is still placed in open foreground in `CAM_ENTRY` and cropped into `CAM_MAIN_ROUTE`; its purpose is clearer, but its parked location continues to compete with route composition.
- The foreground panel at `CAM_PINCH` remains oversized and crowds the conveyor throat, even though its control layout is improved.
- Threshold depth remains unresolved, most clearly in `CAM_DISPATCH`, where an almost-black opening dominates the frame despite the current practical-only light setup.
- The hero close-up shows two unexplained ochre marks just above the new hatch. Confirm whether these are intentional pins/indicators; if so, give them coherent mounting and purpose, otherwise remove them.
- The press, tanks, cabinetry and worktables retain broad pristine planes and repeated supports. Detail density still clusters around controls and the hero, leaving surrounding wide views showroom-clean.

## Largest remaining defects

1. **The room-wide shape language still repeats.** In broad shots, the crusher, sorter, processor, dryer and inspection stations often share box housings, green plates, orange faces and black stands. Only the tank and new drum provide distinct primary silhouettes. This weakens the whole production chain and keeps R04 from reading as a fully authored industrial environment.
2. **A few compositions still obstruct themselves.** The foreground cart, projecting panels and black doorway consume important portions of `CAM_ENTRY`, `CAM_MAIN_ROUTE`, `CAM_PINCH` and `CAM_DISPATCH`. The primary aisle is numerically clear but not consistently calm or legible in pixels.
3. **Surface response and use cues remain too uniform.** The palette is sound, but large surfaces are smooth and clean, and localized contact wear is scarce. The worker story reads through isolated small props rather than the whole working floor.
4. **Dark thresholds do not yet show a believable destination.** Existing practical lintels brighten their frames, while the interior beyond remains mostly black. The route stops at a void in the dispatch view.

## Structural and material changes needed to approach 99 within the fixed shell

1. **Use the existing shell as a sequence of authored process bays.** Keep the protected dimensions, door planes and central route, but revise the equipment footprints and silhouettes within those bounds. Give each process stage a different dominant mass and construction logic—hopper, guarded crusher, belt/sorter, vessel, drum, rack/inspection bench—with purposeful legs, braces, access and service faces. Avoid solving gaps by adding more boxes or trim. Let the machines occupy a few denser, readable bays and preserve broad quiet wall/floor areas between them.
2. **Resolve the route compositions without changing the route contract.** Park the service cart tight to a designated wall-side service bay, and mount or move the pinch-point control panel to the machine frame so it does not project across the conveyor throat. Rebalance the existing equipment silhouettes so the entry and reverse views show the process line without foreground props competing with it.
3. **Make the hero and secondary process mechanics legible at gameplay distance.** Keep the improved access hatch, then give it plausible hinges/clamps and a surrounding service stance. Show where material enters and leaves the tank and drum. Differentiate gauges, valves, guarding and operating state by machine function; simplify any detail that disappears at route distance.
4. **Add purposeful material variation and localized use, not blanket grime.** Separate painted enamel, bare metal, glass, rubber and concrete through roughness/value response visible at 960×540. Add restrained wear where hands, maintenance tools, loaded containers and wheels actually contact surfaces; use broad repair marks and route scuffs sparingly. Keep the new ore as a clearly contained load with material scale that holds up against the stylized industrial forms.
5. **Improve threshold depth using only visible in-room practicals.** Extend the existing lintel approach with physically present bulkhead or task fixtures mounted inside the room at the relevant openings, with visible lenses and local falloff. Use those fixtures to reveal a believable threshold, nearby door/frame and receiving context while preserving world strength zero. Tune each practical's output to produce localized hierarchy rather than flattening all surfaces.
6. **Spread a few useful worker cues across the process.** Continue from the cleaner ledger, cart and service shelf: place one or two plausible tools, replacement components or handled records at the specific machine being serviced. Keep props clustered at work points, show their support/contact, and leave the center aisle and quieter wall areas clear.
