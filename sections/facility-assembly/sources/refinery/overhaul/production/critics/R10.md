# Independent correction-cycle review — R10

**Reviewer:** independent GPT-6 Luna critic

**Scope:** all 11 completed R10 fixed views, directly compared with R09 and reviewed against the refinery brief, Critical Shift art direction, owner rubric, and Spawn quality-reference renders. Technical evidence includes `build_R10.json`, `validation_R10.json`, and `critics/technical_R10.md`.

**Decision:** **R10 is a clear improvement in attachment reliability and several process-machine details, but remains below the owner's ≥99-per-category and weighted ≥99 acceptance threshold.** The service-tag writing is seated again, the crusher mouth has a more legible folded surround, and the dryer/process connection reads more mechanically. The broad machine family still leans on repeated box housings and clean planes; the optical reader is still a generic roof-and-post gate, and route marking competes with the drain. Scores reflect the current R10 images and audit only.

## R09-to-R10 change assessment

**Improvements confirmed in the R10 pixels and audit:**

- The hatch writing and approval mark are back on the paper in `CAM_HERO_DETAIL`. The audit finds their target-ray gaps under 30 µm and confirms the wire reaches both the clip and tag.
- The ear-cup/pad ellipses are correctly oriented vertically: the source audit measures 120 × 186 mm cups and a 1.553 height/width ratio.
- `CAM_MINE_TO_CRUSHER` shows a more resolved crusher mouth with teeth inside the opening, a folded surround, a recessed lip and shoes. The new surround does not close the material path.
- A formed coupling and hose now connect the processor nozzle and dryer inlet, giving the interstage path more construction logic in `CAM_PROCESS` and `CAM_HERO_DETAIL`.
- The fold-edged feed roof is seated along the crusher hood sides/back. The validator also covers more support contacts and closed meshes while preserving all interfaces.

**Defects that remain or regress:**

- The major silhouettes of the reader and surrounding process cabinets are largely unchanged. Its compact scale is better than the early version, but `CAM_PINCH` still shows a generic rectangular roof, dark plate and four posts over the belt.
- Pale control faces in `CAM_ASSEMBLY`/`CAM_DISPATCH` are very conspicuous against orange machinery, while the crusher face is nearly black. The contrast improves button visibility but the changing face colors do not yet read as a consistent control-material system.
- `CAM_MAIN_ROUTE` and `CAM_ENTRY` still show a warm, broad room wash. The practical fixtures are physically motivated and clear; their brightest lenses are close to clipped white, while wall/floor surfaces remain fairly even.
- The large open port fields remain dark in standalone views. These are preserved map interfaces, not missing architecture. Prior R08 connected-map views show map space beyond the openings, though the dispatch-side space is dim.
- R10's feed-hood roof has observed contact against the sides/back but lacks a support registry entry; this is a reproducibility/validator-coverage limitation, not an observed floating part.

## Camera-by-camera defects

- **CAM_ENTRY:** the crusher-mouth opening and process row read clearly, and the clear KEEP CLEAR foreground is useful route space. The large `02` wall stamp remains a dominant flat mark, and the process machines still repeat rectangular control plates and pedestal forms.
- **CAM_MAIN_ROUTE:** the room remains easy to traverse visually, with a clear width across the foreground. Drain grating, aisle dashes and dark equipment bases compete as parallel stripes; the ceiling practicals clip bright white. Preserve the route space while simplifying and aligning its graphic/structural lines.
- **CAM_PROCESS:** the PV and dryer have improved interstage mechanical logic, and the vessel shading is smoother than the R09 blotch. The white controller face is high-contrast, the stage captions are too small for play distance, and the downstream machines still use boxy housings and simple legs.
- **CAM_REVERSE:** the material staging, spill-response tools and conveyor entry are coherent. The open ports are almost black in this fixed-room render; keep them open and use threshold returns/room practicals to clarify the boundary, while relying on actual assembled-map views for beyond-port context.
- **CAM_PINCH:** the crusher and belt mouth gain a visible open surround and teeth, but the sorter remains a roof-and-post rectangle with a small dark central panel. Form the hood as a thin bent sensor arch with a recessed optical bar and cable route so it reads as equipment rather than a bare portal; retain belt visibility and clearance.
- **CAM_MATERIAL:** the glass, steel top, ram, controls and canister are distinguishable. The press is still a large rectilinear orange pedestal and top plate; give the load-bearing frame/ram a more formed cast profile and local wear at the work zone. The pale panel could be a quieter light grey/green so the buttons stay legible without becoming the brightest object.
- **CAM_ASSEMBLY:** the bench/filter storage and shelf give this side of the room a service purpose. The nearest white control panel dominates the foreground and the right interface remains a large black cutout; temper the panel's value and evaluate the opening in assembled context rather than closing it.
- **CAM_DISPATCH:** the cart's four canisters remain contained and the nearby wall lamp is physical. The white control plate competes with the canister cart, the high cabinet and wall are bare, and the port takes much of the image as a black shape; the neighboring map space exists but is dim in the available context render.
- **CAM_MINE_TO_CRUSHER:** the receiving ore, diagonal belt, open crusher mouth, teeth and folded surround tell a clear input sequence. The belt still reads as a long plain slat conveyor, and the green-to-near-black control-face shift gives the foreground box a heavy dark patch. Add a distinctive formed belt support and use a consistent desaturated control finish.
- **CAM_WORK_NOOK:** the shift board, radio, mug and food continue to function as a compact break/work spot. Its papers are faint and the worker cluster is visually unchanged; retain the intentional quiet wall and improve the board's gameplay-distance hierarchy with fewer, larger legible notes.
- **CAM_HERO_DETAIL:** the checked service card is readable as a signed task tag again, with a connected wire; the hatch/gauge/clamp hierarchy remains a strong close-up. The dryer coupling's dark opening reads as a large uncapped stub at this angle, and small dark shadows remain around the tank fittings. Show a cap or a clear mating hose on the coupling and tune the fitting/fixture relation to keep the pressure-vessel face clean.

## Highest-impact remaining fixes

1. Re-form the sorter in `CAM_PINCH` as a compact bent-metal sensor bridge: thin arch, recessed optical bar, visible cable to its control box, and small service access. Keep the current belt clearance and the lower open sightline. The current four-post roof remains the most generic focal asset.
2. Give the crusher-to-belt structure and long conveyor a distinctive load-bearing construction: folded side rails, a believable drive/tension assembly and guarded return rollers. The new open jaw and teeth are a good start; sharpen and ground the next support system to that same standard.
3. Finish the process-machine family around the PV: form the press/processor pedestal as a legible casting/frame with a ram path and service apertures, and distinguish its broad steel, enamel, rubber and glass surfaces through roughness/value hierarchy plus restrained contact wear at handles and material-transfer points.
4. Unify control-face materials across stations. The near-white plates at `CAM_PROCESS`, `CAM_ASSEMBLY` and `CAM_DISPATCH` pull hard focus while the crusher panel turns almost black; a consistent desaturated service-panel material with clean button color contrast would preserve readability and palette coherence.
5. Finish the physical details with a capped or visibly mated dryer coupling in `CAM_HERO_DETAIL`, a support-registry entry for the seated feed-roof edges, and a final check of fixture highlights and small PV shadows. Keep every light tied to its in-room physical fixture.
