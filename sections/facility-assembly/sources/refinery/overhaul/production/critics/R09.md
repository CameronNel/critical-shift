# Independent correction-cycle review — R09

**Reviewer:** independent GPT-6 Luna critic

**Scope:** all 11 completed R09 fixed views, compared with R08 and reviewed against the refinery brief, Critical Shift art direction, owner rubric, and Spawn quality-reference renders. Technical evidence includes `build_R09.json`, `validation_R09.json`, and `critics/technical_R09.md`. The R08 connected-map views are noted separately and do not receive R09 fixed-room credit.

**Decision:** **R09 makes useful construction and material gains, but remains below the owner's ≥99-per-category and weighted ≥99 acceptance threshold.** The supported worktop, lighting, and tag-wire contacts are substantially cleaner. The signed tag has lost its visible text, the PV face still picks up irregular cast shadows, and the process-line silhouettes and surfaces remain less authored than the Spawn reference. Scores judge current R09 pixels and source evidence only.

## R08-to-R09 change assessment

**Improvements visible in R09:**

- The PV service ledge ribs now meet the actual worktop, the filter access sheet seats against the cabinet, and the diffuser panes have retainers and connected support paths. The yoke Boolean stack has been corrected in the saved source.
- The PV service-tag wire now seats at both ends and follows the hatch; a formed carrier puts its instrument details on recognizable hardware. The physical task lights continue to light the vessel from the front girder.
- `CAM_MATERIAL` shows a clearer stainless finish at the press worktop and a readable inner vessel through its glass guard.
- `CAM_DISPATCH` now has a cart with contained canisters, giving that staging corner a useful material-handling task.
- World strength remains zero and all 21 physical room lights pass the latest aperture-ray screen.

**Regressions or unresolved defects:**

- Hatch lettering and the approval mark that were visible in R08 have disappeared from the paper face. The independent audit traces them to coordinates assigned in the rotated hatch's local space, placing them about 4.29 m above the tag.
- Irregular dark shadow forms remain on the red PV body in `CAM_PROCESS` and `CAM_HERO_DETAIL`. The new instrument carrier is seated, but its relation to the practicals and small fittings makes the hero shell read as blotched rather than smoothly formed.
- The R09 scanner remains a flat rectangular housing on four posts in `CAM_PINCH`. Its scale is improved, though the geometry still feels like a generic gate rather than process-specific optical equipment.
- Broad steel, plaster, cabinet and wall surfaces remain mostly immaculate; the improvements to the press top and PV support are localized rather than a room-wide material pass.
- The ear defenders do not yet read as vertically elongated cups; the source audit measures approximately 186 mm across and 120 mm high.
- In the separate R08 connected-map renders, the actual port-adjacent environment is present, so black standalone openings should not be described as missing room geometry. The dispatch-side receiving space remains very dim and barely legible in context.

## Camera-by-camera defects

- **CAM_ENTRY:** the process row reads clearly and the mounted task lights are evident. The large green `02` wall mark draws attention away from the machinery; several station labels are too fine for gameplay distance. The broad KEEP CLEAR foreground is coherent route space and should remain open.
- **CAM_MAIN_ROUTE:** the clear aisle and machine work zone have good separation. Dashed aisle marks, the grated drain and dark equipment plinths make competing parallel lines; unify their spacing and contrast without populating the useful clear lane.
- **CAM_PROCESS:** the tank, dryer and connecting pipework make a legible process cluster. Dark projected patches on the PV body interrupt the enamel surface, the surrounding labels are tiny, and the machine row still repeats rectangular housings and simple footed tables.
- **CAM_REVERSE:** the receiving cart, tool/work area and conveyor landing support the route story. The two open ports are near-black in this isolated view; preserve their open geometry, but make the jamb/floor threshold readable with the room's practical lighting. Context renders confirm adjacent map space but show it dimly.
- **CAM_PINCH:** the smaller reader restores some air around the belt, but the cream roof, black face plate and four posts still form a generic gantry. The belt, crusher discharge and sorter compete at the crossing; use a thinner formed arch, recessed sensor bar and process-specific guard while keeping sight of the material path.
- **CAM_MATERIAL:** the stainless top, glass guard and raised control face are a step forward. The press remains a large orange block on a dark base with a broad control plate; add a formed hydraulic frame and restrained contact wear at the ram/work zone rather than surface noise.
- **CAM_ASSEMBLY:** bench, filter storage and tool shelf imply a usable service work area. The tall dark port on the right still dominates that edge in the standalone crop; the wall and floor remain very clean, while the far process units use similar rectangular panel language.
- **CAM_DISPATCH:** the canister cart is contained and reads as useful worker equipment. The upper wall/cabinet is mostly empty, and the open port occupies a large black field; contextual geometry exists beyond it but is underlit. Improve receiving-zone visibility through existing practicals and threshold returns, not by closing the port.
- **CAM_MINE_TO_CRUSHER:** the ore load and receiving hopper give the belt a clear origin; the strong diagonal helps material-flow reading. The cropped hopper lettering and green hanging control are untidy at this framing, and the long exposed slat conveyor remains a repeated simple silhouette.
- **CAM_WORK_NOOK:** the shift board, radio, mug and food establish a compact break/work corner. Paper notes are pale and low-contrast at this distance, and the snack cluster is the only strongly legible worker cue; strengthen one useful worker interaction detail rather than scattering props across the aisle.
- **CAM_HERO_DETAIL:** the access ring, clamp hardware, gauges and carrier make a convincing focal machine. The tag wire is now connected, but the card itself is blank because its writing is displaced; dark angular shadows still disrupt the vessel enamel. Re-seat the lettering in hatch-local coordinates and check the fixture/fitting shadow shape in this view.

## Highest-impact remaining fixes

1. Correct the hatch label transform using a target world matrix or paper-face coordinates transformed into the hatch's local space. Verify the writing and approval mark sit directly on the tag in `CAM_HERO_DETAIL`, and keep them readable without adding more signage.
2. Rework the PV instrument carrier and its small-gauge/indicator relationship to the front practicals so hardware casts legible contact shadows onto its own brackets, not large irregular dark shapes across the tank shell. Keep only physical fixtures inside the room as light sources; tune their mounting, aperture, and reflector aim, then inspect `CAM_PROCESS` and `CAM_HERO_DETAIL` together.
3. Replace the reader's roof-and-post box in `CAM_PINCH` with a thin, folded sensor arch and recessed optical bar, with the sensor and cable paths visibly built into the guard. Preserve the current compact scale, belt visibility, clearance and route.
4. Extend the formed construction and material hierarchy across the crusher discharge, slat conveyor and dryer: give each a distinct load-bearing support and process-specific guarding, sharpen key folds and contact edges, and add restrained wear at handles, material transfer and service points. Avoid uniformly adding roughness or decorative clutter.
5. Correct the headset cup axes so each ellipse is taller than wide, then verify the pair in a close framing and that both remain visibly supported. Preserve the useful open circulation and route negative space; invest new worker detail only at the existing nook/service stations.
