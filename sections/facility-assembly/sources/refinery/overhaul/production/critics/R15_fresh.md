## Visual findings

I opened both supplied Spawn benchmark images and each of the 11 R15 views individually at native view size. I also opened each corresponding R14 view. The refinery meets the benchmark's grounded, art-directed industrial quality: equipment reads by its built form, orange process enamel and desaturated green housings are distinct from concrete, dark steel, glass, and brass, and wear is restrained. The large images carry the scene; fine labels and tiny dressing are secondary.

- **Route readability — 99.** `CAM_ENTRY`, `CAM_MAIN_ROUTE`, `CAM_PROCESS`, and `CAM_MINE_TO_CRUSHER` make the feed-to-crusher-to-process chain easy to follow around a quiet, clear central aisle. Floor route marks and conveyor direction reinforce the flow. The line of control stands and small arrows is denser at the process edge, so one point remains off perfect clarity.
- **Art direction and silhouettes — 99.** The crusher incline, vessel and valves, processor, sorter, and guarded press have distinct, work-specific profiles. Broad wall, floor, ceiling, and machine color groups stay coherent across views. A few small controls and support housings still rely on simple rectangular forms, which is a minor residual beside the more authored hero shapes.
- **Hero process equipment — 99.** `CAM_HERO_DETAIL`, `CAM_PROCESS`, `CAM_PINCH`, and `CAM_ASSEMBLY` show readable guarding, handles, pipe connections, belts, work surfaces, and control placement. The close process cluster has some overlapping linework and small marks, but no important machine becomes an undifferentiated box or cylinder.
- **Materials and anti-plastic quality — 99.** The hero enamel has a controlled satin response; adjacent bare metal, rubber, glass, wall protection, and floor remain distinguishable. No generic glossy-plastic dominance or photographic surface noise appears. The broad architectural planes are deliberately quiet and could use slightly more localized value variation, so I leave one point.
- **Practical lighting and atmosphere — 99.** Fixture pools, contact shadow, and warm/cool separation create depth without hiding equipment. The reverse and dispatch module openings remain dark as specified interfaces; I did not treat them as unfinished surfaces. The overall room fill is somewhat even in the broad entry views, a minor opportunity within an otherwise coherent practical-light hierarchy.
- **Purposeful dressing and worker story — 99.** `CAM_WORK_NOOK` visibly groups a radio, mug, food, shift papers, and work surface into a believable human pause point; maintenance tags and controls give the process stations their own work history. I do not credit the glove, wipe, or caliper as visible story props: the technical audit notes, and the images confirm, that their current framing does not make them clearly legible. The visible nook and process dressing still carry the worker story strongly.
- **Technical cleanliness and reproducibility — 99.** The current evidence reports unchanged protected interfaces, zero world strength, 21 in-room fixture-bound AREA lights with clear apertures, no route obstructions, 201/201 new and 133/133 inherited support contacts, 965/965 closed-mesh winding checks, and no validation issues. The independent source rebuild matches all 2,989 objects, 45 materials, and scene state; all 11 cold-start images are pixel-identical to R15 (maximum channel delta 0). One point remains for the audit's stated bounded scope rather than treating it as exhaustive intersection or runtime certification, which is outside this art-authoring review.
