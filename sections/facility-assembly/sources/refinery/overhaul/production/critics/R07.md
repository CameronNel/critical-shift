## R06-to-R07 change assessment

**Improvements confirmed in R07:**

- The SO-04 housing is smaller and recessed compared with R06. `CAM_PINCH` shows more of the crossing and belt, though the scan head still occupies a substantial fraction of the image.
- The orange pressure-vessel body reads more evenly in `CAM_HERO_DETAIL`; the unusual R06 dark patches are substantially reduced. The audit confirms radial normals on the cylinder are aligned to the surface. Follow-up high-sample pixels identify the remaining dark shapes as cast shadows from the small gauges/indicator under back-positioned practicals.
- The press base now reads as a cast hydraulic foundation with a sole and service cover instead of a generic thin table/frame (`CAM_MATERIAL`, `CAM_ASSEMBLY`). The worktop and base form a clearer physical stack.
- The dryer now has concave saddles, foot supports and hold-downs that make its support/thermal-cover stack legible in the visible process views; the audit confirms contact and registered support paths.
- Console corners are sharper and the panels show more specific folded construction (`CAM_MATERIAL`, `CAM_PINCH`).
- Physical lens apertures are no longer blocked by wall-lamp housings or the press crown. The thermal cover mounts and previously unsupported pads have current declared contact paths; the auditor independently checked these fixes.

**Defects that remain:**

- The sorter scan head still takes too much visual mass in the close pinch camera and makes the conveyor crossing feel crowded. The small amber sensor/status face helps identify its role, but the surrounding ivory enclosure is still large and box-like.
- The side-collar gauge still appears oversized for its exhaust/collar mount in `CAM_HERO_DETAIL`; reduce or remount it to relate to the other pressure instruments.
- The machine family remains too similar: broad boxes, flat green control faces, orange panels and simple black support legs repeat across route views.
- Surfaces remain clean and smooth, with little selective wear to imply handling, moving ore, repair or repeated work.
- Open map ports continue to read as near-black voids in `CAM_DISPATCH` and `CAM_ASSEMBLY` in standalone renders; preserve them open, but improve the local return/fixture read and evaluate assembly continuity in the full-map context.
- The angled practical heads are physically clear but visually prominent in wide views. Their white apertures and supports need to read as purposeful industrial fittings within the ceiling service rhythm.

## Camera-by-camera defects

- **CAM_ENTRY:** the aisle marking remains clear and the smaller scanner sits better in the process line. Two angled bright task heads stand out near the ceiling; the tank/line is still a repeated row of equipment silhouettes.
- **CAM_MAIN_ROUTE:** more belt and route are visible than in R06. The floor line, drain grid and machine bases compete as three separate directional stripes; several angled luminaires remain visually loud.
- **CAM_PROCESS:** the vessel surface reads cleaner and the hatch is still the hero. The dryer and downstream cabinets retain smooth box/cylinder forms; overhead task fixtures pull focus from the process line.
- **CAM_REVERSE:** the work/safety cluster and route marks read; the conveyor remains a dominant diagonal. The open personnel and mine passages are nearly black beyond the lit returns.
- **CAM_PINCH:** reduced scan head exposes more belt, but its broad white shell still crowds the grade frame and leaves little breathing room over the transfer. The control panel remains a large foreground slab.
- **CAM_MATERIAL:** the hydraulic casting and bevelled console edge improve specific construction. The press glass is readable, but the large table surfaces remain clean, smooth and lightly differentiated.
- **CAM_ASSEMBLY:** scanner cheeks now sit on visible pads and the hydraulic casting supports the table. Shelves and screen add structure, but the open port at right remains dark and machine forms repeat.
- **CAM_DISPATCH:** cylinder cart and rejection control are readable; the black open port consumes much of the background. Local wall light does not reveal a destination beyond it in the standalone view.
- **CAM_MINE_TO_CRUSHER:** the receiving ore and belt convey flow, and the smaller scan unit is clearer. The receiving hopper/conveyor still dominate the left side and the large upper wall lacks worker/process-specific content.
- **CAM_WORK_NOOK:** the shift board is cleaner and the radio/mug/food cluster remains a useful human cue. The cluster occupies a small area against a large quiet wall, and its fine text will not read at gameplay distance.
- **CAM_HERO_DETAIL:** the radial normals are correct and the tank surface is smoother than R06. Remaining dark patches are cast shadows from the small gauges/indicator under back-positioned practicals. The side-collar gauge is too large and sits without a clearly specific bracket or relation to the tank's other gauges; vessel and adjacent drum are cropped tightly.

## Highest-impact remaining fixes

1. Reduce the scan head to a sensor housing and guard sized to the belt, then recheck `CAM_PINCH`, `CAM_ENTRY` and `CAM_MINE_TO_CRUSHER` together. Preserve the scan cue while restoring negative space around the sorter and material transfer.
2. Redesign the crusher, sorter and dryer primary silhouettes to break the repeated cabinet/plate/leg pattern. Use distinct frames, guards, transfer faces, service doors and supports that remain readable from the fixed route views.
3. Move the hero practicals to a front/upper position on the existing girder and soften their visible apertures enough to avoid hard gauge/indicator shadows across the PV05 surface. Keep the small instruments readable and retain believable, motivated form shading; the current radial normals are measured as correct. Remount or resize the exhaust-side gauge and make the PV05 instrument set communicate distinct pressure/temperature/status roles.
4. Add restrained, localized wear to enamel edges, press controls, service handles, the ore route and trolley contact zones. Give rubber, bare metal, glass and concrete more obvious roughness separation at the review resolution.
5. Refine the in-room practical fixtures' support/housing rhythm so the clear lenses feel built into the ceiling/wall system rather than bright detached shapes. Keep all light sources inside the room and bound to visible fixtures; use local returns to improve thresholds while preserving the open ports.
6. Add a few specific work traces to process/inspection stations—handled records, replacement parts, tool placement or a recently serviced component—so the broad room views express active workers without filling quiet areas with clutter.
