## Visual assessment

R13 preserves R12's strong route and machine hierarchy. `CAM_ENTRY`, `CAM_MAIN_ROUTE`, `CAM_REVERSE` and `CAM_MINE_TO_CRUSHER` keep the 2.8 m aisle clear and make the receiving-to-crusher route legible. The large dark openings in `CAM_REVERSE` and `CAM_DISPATCH` remain adjacent-map module interfaces; I do not treat them as unfinished dressing. The clear floor is intentional circulation space. Compared with R12, the broad palette, lighting rhythm and composition are stable, and the localized corrections have not damaged the route.

The hero processor reads a little more cleanly now that the riser no longer has the obvious pale wedge. The orange pressure vessel, top band, pressure dial, clamped round access hatch, valves, curved product line and green drive housing make a convincing focal cluster. The technical corrections to the hollow line, hose and fittings improve the construction story. The close cameras still show a simplified object language: large curved vessels and broad covers do most of the work, while details such as the annular bore and smaller attachment transitions are hidden at this camera scale. I do not see a remaining riser defect or a tank-surface patch that warrants the earlier surface-defect finding.

Painted steel, enamel, glass and darker structural members stay visually distinct, with controlled highlights and little surface noise. R13's edge work and riser correction improve polish slightly, but the main material views are nearly unchanged from R12. In particular, the press and sorter sheets still read as large flat faces in `CAM_MATERIAL` and `CAM_PINCH`; the audited returns exist but do not visibly change their fabricated character enough to earn a 99. The current treatment is coherent and matte-to-semi-matte, not generic glossy plastic.

Practical lighting remains successful and unchanged in character. The R13 validation reports world strength zero, 21 in-room AREA lights, 105 five-point samples without unintended obstruction, no route obstructions and no issue entries. The renders show localized warm pools and a useful dark-to-light hierarchy. There is no visible fill wash or unsupported luminous source.

The detail changes around workers and maintenance are purposeful, and the crusher record is a sensible addition near an access door. At fixed gameplay distance, however, these are small increments: the card is not readable in the broader views and the glove is not a clear new focal detail. The earlier Spawn comparison remains a stronger example of readable human use grouped around work surfaces. R13 has not yet distributed enough clearly visible, role-specific worker cues through the refinery to close that gap.

## Three strongest visual changes toward 99

1. **Make the central sorter optical plate and its folded housing read as manufactured layers.** In `CAM_PINCH` (R13, around x=620–930, y=125–360 of the 960×540 frame), the white sorter head is still a broad angular cover and the central dark sensor plate hangs on a plain vertical bar. Give the plate a clearly bounded inset or glass face, visible folded return/depth at the sides, and a distinct mount that explains its function. Preserve the two rear cantilevers and the current readable silhouette.
2. **Make the press and calibration-cover returns visible at their review angles.** In `CAM_MATERIAL` (R13, around x=250–660, y=240–470), the green access panel and control surface remain flat, with the side returns mostly hidden by the viewing angle. Reposition or shape returns so the folds, flange depth and panel overlap read from this fixed camera. The source-level return meshes pass support checks, but that alone does not close the visual fabrication gap.
3. **Strengthen work evidence at the stations where people use the equipment.** Keep the nook quiet and preserve the clear aisle. Increase the fixed-camera readability of the crusher record and alignment glove, and add one or two supported, operation-specific details at the crusher or inspection station so the human story is visible in `CAM_ENTRY`, `CAM_PINCH`, `CAM_ASSEMBLY` or `CAM_DISPATCH` without relying on small print. Current additions are plausible but too subtle to shift the room-wide impression materially.
