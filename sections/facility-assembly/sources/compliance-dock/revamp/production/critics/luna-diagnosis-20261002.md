# Independent visual diagnosis — compliance dock, full cycle 02

**Review scope:** pixel review of all 27 `full-cycle-02` renders and all 4 `spawn-reference` images, against the current overhaul brief, art direction, build protocol and local rubric. This is a pre-repair visual diagnosis. Scores below are independent estimates from the rendered evidence, not measured technical results. A category must be strictly above 93 to pass the local rubric.

## Highest-impact visible fixes

1. **Replace the repeated beveled-box read on the inspection machinery with object-specific construction.** This is the dominant art-direction failure and repeats in the most important frames: `C01_ENTRY`, `C02_HERO_DOCK`, `C04_SCANNER_APPROACH`, `HERO_SCANNER`, `C05_CONVEYOR_LEAD_TUNNEL`, `HERO_CARGO`, `C06_CART_GATE_G1`, and `C09_ARRIVAL_GATE_P2`. The scanner uprights, cart gate, cargo tunnel, and arrival leaf panels are mostly large planar blue/navy masses with shallow outline panels, regular chamfers, or black cutouts. They read like a coordinated kit of chunky props rather than manufactured inspection equipment. Keep all existing machine envelopes, openings, operating lanes, and placements fixed. Rebuild the visible surfaces as fabricated assemblies: a distinct load-bearing frame, folded sheet-metal skins with returns and seams, inset service covers with credible hinges/latches, gasketed inspection apertures, guarded controls, and selective steel/rubber/plastic material breaks. Use different edge treatments for the scanner's structural steel and the thinner enclosure panels. Add only construction details that explain assembly or maintenance; avoid decorative greebling.

2. **Make check-in visibly function as a worker handover station.** `C03_CHECKIN_COUNTER` and `DETAIL_CHECKIN` show a broad, dark counter crowded with a paper stack, clipboard, stamp-like object, tray and small loose tools, while the sign and display are distant or visually weak. From the room view the purpose reads as a desk behind a hatch, not the required controlled check-in/evidence handoff. Preserve the counter, hatch, office glazing and footprint. Give the hatch a stronger, legible transaction edge: a deliberate pass-through sill, a distinct document intake slot/tray integrated into the counter, a place for the worker's ID/evidence to be set down, and a reachable staff-side record surface. Recompose the existing papers and tools into one clear handoff sequence instead of a scatter. Make the sign and monitor support the function at normal view distance, with legible restrained wording and a readable screen state. Keep details subordinate to the inspection arch and avoid adding more wall labels.

3. **Fix the light hierarchy and lift important forms out of near-black.** `C01_ENTRY`, `C02_HERO_DOCK`, `C04_SCANNER_APPROACH`, `C05_CONVEYOR_LEAD_TUNNEL`, `C08_CONCEALED_SUPPORT_H1`, `C10_ROOF_SERVICES`, and the four corner views show very dark ceilings, deep black machinery recesses, and important lower/side surfaces falling into shadow. The office in `C07_OFFICE_INTERIOR` is separately warm and strongly isolated, so the dock and office feel like unrelated lighting setups. The small bright fixtures do not consistently explain the illumination on nearby equipment. Retain the existing fixture positions and circulation. Shape the inspection bay with coherent practical pools that reveal the scanner's frame, cargo aperture and conveyor supports; retain darker secondary storage but lift the critical silhouettes and contact areas. Use restrained cool industrial light at inspection and a related, softer warm practical in the office/check-in zone. Provide a low level of indirect fill so structural detail remains readable, and tune fixture output/falloff so the light sources visibly affect adjacent surfaces without washing out the scene.

4. **Give the cargo tunnel and test load a readable mechanical/material story.** In `C05_CONVEYOR_LEAD_TUNNEL` and `HERO_CARGO`, the giant blue tunnel is a plain housing around a black rectangle; the object being inspected is a clean, nearly cubical red-and-blue box with a label that is too small/soft to clarify its construction. The visible roller bed looks like repeated gray cylinders on a dark frame, and the hand crank is a yellow peg. Preserve tunnel aperture, belt/roller path, supports, and test-load position. Define the tunnel as an inspection unit with a framed and sealed mouth, layered inner shielding/curtain or sensor elements, accessible side service panels, and a clear infeed/outfeed transition. Give the load the seams, corner protection, closure/latch logic and restrained handling wear of a reusable industrial case. Show enough roller shafts, bearings and side rails to explain how it moves; make the crank a connected working control rather than a loose visual accent. Keep the case's silhouette and scale plausible against the existing belt.

5. **Add a few authored traces of a pressured, active workplace and reduce the uninterrupted blankness.** `WALL_SOUTH` and `WALL_EAST` are mostly large unarticulated surfaces; `WALL_WEST` has a long bare section; `C01_ENTRY`, `CORNER_NE`, `CORNER_NW` and `CORNER_SE` show sparse peripheral dressing. There are a few useful clues—the covered transfer trolley, the paper at evidence storage, the office desk—but most surfaces and machines remain uniformly clean and staged. The spawn references (`VALIDATE_Spawn`, `VALIDATE_LockerDoor`, `VALIDATE_Material_A`, `BRIEFING_INDIRECT`) have warmer rust/orange accents, human-scale locker/bench groupings and a stronger sense of daily use. Preserve the dock's approved layout and restrained industrial palette. Introduce shallow architectural articulation to the blank walls (panel joints, a few access covers, believable kick/base transitions) without filling every wall. Add a small number of localized traces tied to inspection and maintenance—handled paperwork, a worn route edge, a patched or replaced panel, a tool left at a repair—clustered around existing functional areas. Bring a muted warm accent or practical-light response into the dock in a few controlled places so the palette relates to spawn; retain blue as the inspection identifier and do not turn the room into a color-saturated prop set.
