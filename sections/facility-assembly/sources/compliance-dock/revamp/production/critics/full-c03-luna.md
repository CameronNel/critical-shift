## Critical visual defects

1. **Repeated primitive panel construction dominates key equipment.** The scanner arch, cargo inspection machine, cart gate, arrival door and multiple cabinets use similar box shells and inset rectangles with limited object-specific construction. This is visible in C02, C05, C06, C09, HERO_SCANNER and HERO_CARGO, and conflicts with the art direction's hard anti-blockout and repeated-bevelled-box gates.
2. **Close-range operational graphics are not finished.** Text on cargo and check-in papers/signs visibly collapses into broken marks in C03, C05, HERO_CARGO and DETAIL_CHECKIN. These are focal inspection and check-in views, so the issue reads as damaged artwork rather than intentional small print.
3. **The concealed support station is not legible at its intended focal view.** C08 and HERO_TROLLEY show a tarp-covered form against a dark wall; its support equipment, evidence function and narrative details are difficult to distinguish. The low light hides the design instead of making concealment purposeful.

## Highest-impact repairs within the preserved layout

1. Redesign the scanner arch, cargo inspection assembly and cart gate through object-specific secondary forms: believable joints, guards, access doors, cable/service paths and operational interfaces. Keep current footprints and openings; avoid adding another layer of generic inset rectangles.
2. Rework close-up signage and paperwork at C03, C05 and DETAIL_CHECKIN so the few focal words and forms read cleanly at the supplied render size. Remove or replace visibly broken microcopy and give each sheet a clear physical role.
3. Rebalance key lighting at H1 and under the roof structure. Let practical pools reveal the trolley, evidence storage and machine faces while retaining darker secondary space; reduce the broad black ceiling mass where it overwhelms the room.
4. Bring the approved spawn color relationship into the dock with a measured warm institutional base and intentional rust/orange blocks alongside navy equipment. Separate painted metal, concrete, rubber, glass and paper by visible response; use localized wear only where contact explains it.
5. Give the work areas a few specific human traces and repaired details—such as a used clipboard or docket, a maintenance patch, misplaced seal stock, or a handled tool—clustered near their related task. Retain quiet wall areas; the current empty elevations need purposeful rhythm rather than general prop fill.
