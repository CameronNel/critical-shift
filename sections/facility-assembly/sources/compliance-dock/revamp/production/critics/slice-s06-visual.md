# Style slice s06 — independent visual review

**Verdict: LOCAL FAIL. Expansion remains blocked.**

Review date: 2026-10-01. Scope: front-office wall/transition, D1 staff door, check-in counter cluster, task practical, junction/conduit and worker forms/pen/stamp/tray/cloth/contact traces. Inherited room context is excluded from the verdict. This is a fresh pixel review; no builder reasoning, earlier reviews or implementation history were consulted. No full-room score or acceptance is issued. Technical cleanliness, envelope preservation, measured scale, support contact, dependencies and reproducibility are unassessed.

The slice establishes a credible check-in function, restrained off-white/navy/orange colour grouping and a bleak institutional tone. The staff door has readable framing, inset panels, lever/plate and kick protection; the counter has a service opening, protective glass/intercom and useful paperwork. The practical creates a warm pool against the cooler office and dark dock. The sparse signs carry the coercive story well. The remaining finish is below the polished style-slice bar: the cloth is visually a flat sheet, rubber/plastic identity is insufficiently distinct, and most handling surfaces remain conspicuously pristine. The protocol requires those qualities to be demonstrated before expansion.

## Camera findings

**SLICE_ENTRY:** The station and staff door are clear before reading text. The large window/service opening and contrasting door give an intelligible functional hierarchy. The practical warms the lintel and counter region while the adjoining facility is darker, so flat-even-lighting is not the dominant read. The reassurance sign followed by the responsibility clause communicates coercion with little clutter. At player height, however, the small worker traces largely merge into clean rectangles and tiny silhouettes, and the door/counter show little accumulated use. The inherited scanner context is not judged.

**SLICE_MATERIAL:** The glass/intercom, tray, form/clipboard, tethered pen and stamp identify an operated check-in station. Detail density and negative space are disciplined. The metal rim and hardware have controlled highlights, paper is bright and matte, and glass adds reflected depth. The cloth and mat fail the tactile-family demonstration; the neutral view reinforces that finding. The soft reflections on the protective pane are not clear enough to count as unmistakable human fingerprints or handled wear. The background office equipment is inherited context and excluded.

**SLICE_DOOR:** Door scale appears plausible relative to the counter, and its frame, recessed angular panels, hinge positions, lever plate, kickplate and adjacent reader give useful secondary construction. The door avoids a plain primitive slab. Its pristine broad paint and small, faint kickplate scratches fall short of the required visible use. The nearby wall and navy lower protection remain restrained and matte, although low-frequency surface variation is subtle. The floor threshold and lowest door edge are largely cropped; these pixels do not prove bottom sealing or support contact.
