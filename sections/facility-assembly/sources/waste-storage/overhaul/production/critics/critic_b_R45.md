## Visual assessment

R45’s strongest change is its surface treatment. The large angular damage stamps seen during the R41/R42 diagnostics are absent. C02 and W04 now show low-contrast directional coating fade along vessel/collar and carrier zones, without a hard polygon perimeter. C06 and C07 have soft camera-visible variation across their broad lid/front faces. This keeps the equipment readable and preserves the dark, restrained mood. The cask end faces in W10 also read as solid caps rather than black openings.

Materials remain the only below-gate visual category. In C02, broad faces of both SC01 and SC02 still read as large, mostly smooth painted areas; the new directional exposure is visible but limited to faint vertical value shifts. W04’s foreground SC01 body likewise remains substantially even across its broad lit shell. C06 and C07 improve this, but the fade is subtle and leaves adjacent broad surfaces smooth. Against the R24 refinery hero/material references, these primary surfaces carry less visible coating history and material breakup, so the equipment still feels cleaner and more uniform than the requested run-down setting. This is a finish-level deduction, not a defect in the repeated forms, route clearance, or function.

R45 otherwise holds the intended gloomy, localized lighting: shadows remain deep, visible pools stay around modeled practicals, and important equipment and routes remain legible. The repeated cells and casks read as distinct manufactured waste-storage fixtures; the staged trolley, service tools, failed ring, gloves and shift paperwork support use without cluttering the protected route. The clean central spine and segregated approaches remain easy to read. The authoring does not show the generic beveled-box construction, plastic response, flooded lighting, cheerfulness, blocked paths, floating props, or broken material cutouts listed as visual vetoes.
