## Highest-impact repairs

Make the vise, tools and work surface read as authored equipment rather than primitives. Preserve useful negative space and keep this detail localized.
2. **Restore light hierarchy while retaining gloom.** Preserve dark intervals, but give the corner a clearly readable pool from a visible, modeled, supported practical. In C03, lift local contrast around the rear opening and route edge enough to identify the destination and adjacent controls. Do not solve this with broad ambient fill or uniform exposure. C10 alone cannot prove fixture-only illumination because no fixture is visible in that framing.
3. **Make wear specific and material-driven.** Show localized handling wear on tool grips and bench edges, restrained oxidation on exposed steel, residue at the actual sealing task, and dirty concrete distinct from painted metal. The baseline's orange has been muted to brown, but the changed pixels read as desaturation/dimming rather than purposeful neglect.
4. **Add one coherent human repair narrative.** Dress the work surface with a small, supported set of task evidence (for example a tagged failed seal, gloves, repair tape and a shift/service note). Keep the objects grouped around the vise and leave the adjacent surface quiet. Current tools alone do not communicate an active, neglected maintenance process.
5. **Repair the dark rear read in C03.** The opening at the center rear is nearly featureless black, while the far-left zone and labels are also hard to resolve. Define door/frame depth and keep enough light on boundaries to read route, threshold and destination. Maintain the fixed camera for comparison.
