# Luna independent full-corridor review — F7ci

## Decision

**F7ci is a substantial, visible construction pass over F6ci, but it does not pass art acceptance.** All seven category scores and all nine area scores remain below the required strict threshold of greater than 98. The most consequential gains are visible in the route architecture and task-specific fixtures: the entry heat-recovery fins and suspended threshold canopy, formed crossing-wall jackets, a more readable clean-transfer sign, the supported bypass purge/check kit, the linked B/017 waste-bin/receipt labels, and the D05 freight motor over a physically open three-bay lower channel. These are visible geometry and composition changes, not credits for code or cold-validation results.

The remaining gap is still whole-scene authorship. The corridor now has more large forms, but several fixed views continue to present broad smooth wall panels, repeated navy kick walls and ceiling grids around isolated service features. The new jacket and fin language improves that shell without yet giving each section the distinctive threshold composition, layered human-use details and material variation visible across the spawn reference. The F7 C10 water wheel also remains crowded against the ceiling light in projection; the separate F8 draft is outside this evidence set and receives no credit.

The comparison uses the same four actual spawn renders as a fixed 100-point reference. It does not require duplicating irrelevant spawn props or filling every quiet wall. It compares composition, construction, material response, lighting and purposeful human-scale use. I opened all 19 F7ci images at 1280×853, the closed freight-leaf diagnostic, and all four 1280×720 spawn images. I independently verified the F7ci native SHA256 (`30e4ca9bc23c03c87fe460b5754f6abfc438853237e0c4253c7357bddb7ef3cb`), all 19 full-image hashes, and the closed-image hash against their manifests. The four reference PNGs were also hashed and opened. Cold validation reports PASS with zero failures; technical metrics add no art points.

## Remaining repairs, bounded to visible views

1. **Complete the architectural hierarchy across the route.** Keep the real canopy, heat-recovery fins and formed jackets. In `C02/C04/C08`, refine a small number of large wall/ceiling transitions so each process station changes the corridor’s silhouette and value grouping, not only its trim pattern. Break the fin run at useful cassette access/transition points where appropriate; avoid extending uninterrupted repeated seams across every view. Keep the fixed outer footprint and clear route.

2. **Resolve D05’s remaining value problem.** Preserve the visibly open three-bay lower frame and the now-in-frame motor. Use a broad, soft bounce into the rear web and bracket/support faces, especially the dark lower-right bay, while retaining the task-light highlight on the motor. The remaining defect is shadow separation, not the repaired frame mass.

3. **Clarify the visible D06 maintenance operation.** Keep the raised purge/check unit and connected hose. Route the loop so it does not cross the small unit’s face/label, then use restrained contrast or a task cue to make the kit’s purpose readable from this fixed view. This is a targeted readability repair, not a request for general clutter.

4. **Separate the F7 C10 overhead silhouettes.** In the reviewed F7 pixels, the inline WATER wheel sits close to the fluorescent and competes with the PLANT / S01 header. Adjust spacing or light placement so the valve remains visibly attached to its continuous pipe but reads as a distinct control. The later F8 diagnostic is not evidence for this F7 decision.

5. **Use the spawn comparison to strengthen connected work composition without detailing every wall or opening ports.** Spawn also has broad pink/navy fields and repeated floor tiles; its advantage is that framed side openings reveal specific human-use layers—briefing material, locker/PPE storage, a handover board and furniture—within otherwise quiet architecture. Apply that compositional logic selectively: in `C04`, give the long return wall one coherent enclosed maintenance/shift-change node (for example a recessed cart-repair board with mounted tools and a restrained local work light), leaving the adjacent wall and travel lane quiet. In `C05`, tie the reactor-direction beam to the existing louver/return assembly with one readable lockout/inspection point and its connected pipe or cable run, so the turn has a process task as well as a sign. In `C08`, stage the clean corridor’s existing linen/bottle shelf as an identifiable clean-to-used transfer point with a closed-front return cabinet or supported bag and a small area label, keeping the clean wall fields and route open. In `C06/D04`, compose the existing reactor status/isolation panel, attached line and extinguisher as one lit arrival/check station beside the monumental closed leaves; use their current support hardware and a localized pool to make that workflow read at both scales. Preserve the fixed exterior connections as closed portals. These few connected stations address the actual parity gap; blanket wall detailing, extra floor marks or unrelated props would not.

6. **Preserve the successful area-specific fixes.** Keep the matching B/017 bin/receipt link in `E01`, the larger route-readable sign in `C07`, the framed `CLEAN / S02` sign in `E02`, and the formed thermal/jacket architecture. These repairs are legible and should not be reverted while addressing the broader composition.

The closed freight image is a rigid-leaf visual diagnostic. It supports inspection of constructed leaf faces and lit apertures but does not establish runtime motion, collision, continuous sweep, or clearance. The fixed images do not establish Unity lighting/performance or unseen contact behavior. No points are awarded for hidden assets, source claims, or the separate F8 experiments.
