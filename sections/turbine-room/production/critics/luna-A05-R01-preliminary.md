## Actual R01 camera observations

### C01 entry

The entry view establishes the main machine as the dominant right-side mass and leaves a readable lane along the left. Cream shell, charcoal framing and orange route marks are legible, with no teal drift. The machine occupies much of the frame, so the full door/entry relationship and destination read remain dependent on the missing reverse/route views. The pipe crossing toward the west side must be checked against the reported R02 overshoot fix.

### C02 hero

The hero composition gives the casing a strong broad silhouette and shows support pedestals, base and cable routing. It currently reads more as one long cream machine than as a clearly staged HP casing → LP expansion/exhaust → coupling → generator sequence; the absent rear context limits the conversion-train read. Keep the planar silhouette but strengthen the functional transitions and preserve the central lane.

### C06 throttle

The physical levers, red trip button, and output/demand/reserve/safety group establish the intended control vocabulary. The top labels, especially the left cropped `HEALTH` field and pale lettering over pale boards, are too low contrast for fast gameplay reading. The panels also feel like a row of independent modules; the cluster needs stronger grouping and state hierarchy. R02’s backing/contrast correction should be judged in an identical camera.

### C07 coupling

The orange guard has an immediate hazard/readability signal, and the central `GUARD / 01` sign makes the station identifiable. The repeated vertical/horizontal bars are visually uniform and obscure the shaft, so the removable guard construction and access state need a clearer silhouette at gameplay distance. The sign must remain physically attached and readable after the reported floating-text correction.

### C09 generator

The generator reads as a distinct dark-front/cream-ribbed assembly with credible supports and end hardware. The close framing emphasizes the repeated ribs and blank broad shell; one or two functional access/terminal cues would help the service story without adding clutter. The loose cable loops at floor level are visible beside the base and require saved geometry/route validation before they can be treated as safe service dressing.

## Required R02 re-review

R02’s reported fixes address relevant visible risks: pipe overshoot into the west apron, weak control backing/contrast, hoist contact, and slight floating text. Each must be checked in the saved R02 pixels and objective audit; no fix is credited from the description alone. In particular:

- The west-apron pipe must not reduce the designed operator/service apron or intrude into the central keep-clear volume.
- Control labels must remain readable without relying on color alone, and the cluster must retain a clear throttle/load/readback/trip hierarchy.
- Hoist contact must be physically supported and remain within the existing overhead reservation; contact is not a load certification.
- Signs and labels must be attached to their intended panels, with no visible floating elements.
- Re-render the five R01 cameras identically for comparison, then add the five missing cameras before any final category approval.

## Review result

**A05:** graphic/planar no-teal guidance is approved only for the individually passing rows; all sub-90 rows are rejected as approval.  
**R01:** promising first whole-room pass, but preliminary fail in five visible categories and unscored technical status. No final approval is issued. The next valid evidence is R02 pixel review plus saved-file technical validation, followed by all ten fixed cameras and the missing maintenance/material views.
