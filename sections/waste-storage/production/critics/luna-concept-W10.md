## Directly observed defects and limits

- The left side’s casks/overpacks are visually readable as waste storage, but the framing does not establish the planned tall shielded cask size or exact inventory count. Those remain plan authority.
- The dispatch opening is clearly visible in this entry-facing composition, but the scene beyond it is a simple bright corridor/room; no reciprocal receiving view or full route proof is supplied here.
- Several large oxide floor abrasion patches create a repeated graphic motif. They work as broad wear accents, though their regular placement could become decorative if repeated across every camera.
- The repeated chevron mark appears on booth, containers, and wall copy. It reads as an invented facility identity, but the current art prompt forbids logos; keep it treated as restrained department marking rather than letting it become branding-led composition.
- The workbench/tool station at right and hand truck at left are useful human-use evidence, but no worker, cart load, or active inspection state is visible. This is appropriate for a clean environment plate but leaves gameplay state unproven.
- The matte treatment is substantially improved over W02/W03 and aligns with W07. Material identity is clear at the overall scale, while small seals, rubber, and container hardware will need pixel proof in dedicated close-ups.

## Scope decision

W10 is approved only as the current overall visual-direction target: broad graphic matte construction, warm practical pools, clean central route, restrained orange/graphite grouping, and maintained waste-facility storytelling. It does not approve exact cask/container dimensions, cell inventory counts, portal sweeps, electrical seam, utility sockets, support contacts, or runtime readiness. Re-review remains required against the final fixed-camera scene after the complementary entry-zone plate and full objective checks exist.
