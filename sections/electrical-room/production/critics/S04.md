## Highest-impact remaining observed defects

1. **The finish still reads as a clean procedural render.** S01/S02 cabinet faces, S03 floor and door, and S04 cart retain broad uniform surface treatment. The references use more convincing material-specific edge response and selective wear without sacrificing quiet surfaces. S04 remains visibly short of that tactile standard.
2. **Manufactured specificity is uneven at the main focal object.** The opened equipment is substantially more informative, but S02's regular ringed bodies, black cavity, simple front controls and coarse minor hardware do not yet read as a fully resolved industrial assembly. Source complexity cannot fill that gap.
3. **The added wide view reveals an architectural blemish above the entry.** In S03, the long dark horizontal strip above the sliding-door rail appears like a raw slot or rendering artifact against the otherwise finished wall. Its dark/brown interior and abrupt termination do not establish a convincing integrated repair or construction transition. The visible defect is clear; its technical cause has not been verified.
4. **Lighting still suppresses useful material and equipment information.** The overhead bus is conspicuously brighter than the rest of the equipment, while the open cabinet background is nearly black. The new practical fixture makes illumination causality more credible, but the material/working-area hierarchy is still basic.
5. **The maintenance props are readable but lack convincing finish.** In S04, the meter, spare parts, paperwork and rag have recognizable roles, yet their simplified edges, undisturbed arrangement and largely pristine surfaces keep them below the reference's human, maintained-workplace quality. This is a quality shortfall, not a missing-count problem.
