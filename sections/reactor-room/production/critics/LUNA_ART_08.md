# Luna independent art review — Reactor Art08

**Decision: FAIL visual acceptance.** Weighted score: **77.6/100**. All eight categories are below the required 90/100 floor. This is a pixel-based art review; no source, `.blend`, builder, or other task was modified.

## Evidence inspected

- All ten Art08 fixed-camera renders: `01_HERO` through `10_EAST_HIGH`.
- Fresh-reopen diagnostics: `CONTROL_INTERIOR_DIAGNOSTIC`, `PLAN_DIAGNOSTIC`, and `POOL_DEPTH_DIAGNOSTIC`.
- Approved style references: `reference-a02-hall.png` and `reference-b01-controls.png`.
- P02 architecture sheets/specification, Art Direction, camera contract, rubric, and the Art08 render validation manifest.

The approved references were used for material, lighting, construction language and hierarchy only. Their superseded stair composition was not treated as layout authority. Scale and technical scores below are explicitly provisional where a still cannot prove a measured or animated fact.

## Category evidence

### 1. Scale, construction and circulation — 83/100

The `PLAN_DIAGNOSTIC` shows the centered pool, paired bank footprint and east-side control/stair relationship. `04_TURBINE_AISLE`, `05_REVERSE_NORTH` and `10_EAST_HIGH` show an apparently continuous perimeter route with no obvious prop blockage. This is a provisional visual read, not a measurement of the 1.20 m / 1.31 m requirements.

The weak evidence is camera-specific: `07_COMPACT_STAIR` is an extremely tight upward view with the landing and door relationship largely absent, so the compact stair reads as a stair flight rather than a clearly usable route. `06_CONTROL_ROOM` is dominated by a thick foreground sill/rail condition that makes the observation relationship harder to judge. Several large camera crops also remove the surrounding scale context from the banks.

### 2. Shape specificity and art direction — 76/100

The room has the intended broad shell palette and industrial silhouette, but it remains visibly generic beside A02/B01. The two moving carriages in `01_HERO`, `09_BANK_MECHANISMS` and `10_EAST_HIGH` are near-identical teal rectangular housings with surface plates, strips and handles applied to them. Perimeter consoles repeatedly resolve as a dark plinth, a teal box, a small screen, a gauge and a few buttons. Access panels, wall lights and structural trims repeat with little object-specific distinction.

The banks are readable, but their secondary construction is not strong enough to carry the art direction without the `CONTROL BANK A/B` signs. This is the hard failure mode called out by the target: labels and bevels are doing too much of the identity work.

### 3. Reactor hierarchy and focal clarity — 82/100

The primary hierarchy is partly successful. `01_HERO` and `10_EAST_HIGH` clearly show the cyan pool and exactly two dominant bank drives. `POOL_DEPTH_DIAGNOSTIC` proves that the shaft has real visible depth rather than being only a flat cyan disk.

The focal read is not stable across the batch. In `01_HERO`, `05_REVERSE_NORTH`, `09_BANK_MECHANISMS` and `10_EAST_HIGH`, the upper bank supports or housings are cut by the frame. In the hero views the two large columns and guardrails occupy so much of the central field that the lower core becomes a cyan/white glow with limited readable structure. The depth diagnostic has the same problem at close range: the rail bars and columns obscure the lower internals, while the bright lower emitter approaches clipping. The south gate controls in `03_SOUTH_GATE` are visible, but `ACKNOWLEDGE` and `BYPASS` read more clearly than a distinct SCRAM identity; the interaction anchor is not as immediate as in B01.

### 4. Material separation and tactile finish — 72/100

The broad material grouping is correct: pale architectural shell, dark framing/rails, teal machinery, white utility pipes, yellow safety accents, metallic drive columns and cyan pool. `08_MATERIAL_SLICE` is the strongest evidence for this separation.

The finish is still too smooth and uniform. Painted metal and molded plastic share a similar clean teal response; pale wall panels, flooring and pipework lack convincing roughness differences; the floor is almost spotless across all route views. There is very little localized contact wear, edge use, repair evidence or authored mismatch. Compared with both approved references, the current pixels look more like clean procedural assets than maintained, tactile industrial equipment.

### 5. Lighting, depth and exposure — 79/100

The normal state is bright and playable. Wall-mounted practicals create visible pools of light, the shell has some falloff, and primary bases/rails generally cast grounding shadows. The control diagnostic is sufficiently exposed to judge the consoles and chairs.

The lighting rhythm is overly repetitive: identical small wall fixtures and near-identical cones repeat around the shell, creating a patterned evenness rather than a composed hierarchy. The cyan pool is often too bright at its lower source (`01_HERO`, `03_SOUTH_GATE`, `10_EAST_HIGH`, `POOL_DEPTH_DIAGNOSTIC`), bleaching the core read. Conversely, upper support areas and portions of the hall behind the foreground structures lose useful surface detail. The result is bright overall, but not consistently deep or tactile.

### 6. Colour and detail discipline — 74/100

The core palette is disciplined: warm off-white shell, desaturated teal equipment, charcoal structure, white pipes, cyan hero light and restrained yellow controls. This is a genuine strength.

The detail language is less disciplined. Large white floor arrows and the `EMERGENCY SHUTDOWN` floor text are prominent in `01_HERO`, `03_SOUTH_GATE` and `10_EAST_HIGH`; they read closer to an overlay or training mock-up than quiet facility communication. Repeated sector plates, `CONTROL BANK` labels, access-panel outlines and identical lights distribute the same visual emphasis everywhere. Labels remain necessary to identify too many otherwise similar assets, which weakens the quiet-to-focal rhythm required by the target.

### 7. Environmental credibility — 68/100

`CONTROL_INTERIOR_DIAGNOSTIC` is the best human-use evidence: two operator stations, physical switches, gauges, CRT-like displays, chairs, a mug and paperwork are present. `08_MATERIAL_SLICE` also shows a shift-check card and service hardware.

Outside that diagnostic, the hall feels staged and under-used. Large shell areas are blank repeated panels; many machines appear as isolated display objects with limited visible service connection; pipe routes are legible but their endpoints frequently leave the frame or disappear behind equipment, so continuity is not convincing from the fixed views. There is almost no localized maintenance trace, route wear or personal/operator evidence in the production angles. The room reads as a clean asset arrangement with labels rather than a plant actively operated by adults.

### 8. Technical cleanliness and reproduction — 87/100 provisional

All ten named renders and all three fresh-reopen diagnostics are present and visually renderable. The current manifest reports a cold-start pass, and the stills do not show an obvious primary machine floating above its base. Those are positive reproduction observations, not an art-quality pass.

The visible technical presentation is weakened by repeated key-subject cropping: the banks are clipped at the top in several views, and `06_CONTROL_ROOM` has a large foreground architectural/rail obstruction that masks the hall. `07_COMPACT_STAIR` provides very little usable verification context. `POOL_DEPTH_DIAGNOSTIC` is useful but its railing and drive columns block a substantial portion of the subject. Still images cannot establish correct pivots, separation, animation states or dependency behavior; this score must remain provisional pending the independent technical audit.

## Camera-specific observed defects

- **01_HERO:** upper bank assemblies are cropped; columns and rail consume the center; lower pool source trends toward white; oversized floor arrows/text compete with the focal subject.
- **02_WEST_ENTRY:** entry lane is readable, but the pool is pushed to the edge and the view offers little evidence of the hero hierarchy; the equipment reads clean and smooth rather than tactile.
- **03_SOUTH_GATE:** gate, acknowledgement and bypass are visible, but the separate SCRAM read is weak; foreground rail hides the pool; the upper bank structures are out of frame.
- **04_TURBINE_AISLE:** useful route/material evidence, but the turbine is cut at the right edge and the pipe/equipment relationship is only partially observable; the floor and shell remain nearly unused-looking.
- **05_REVERSE_NORTH:** reverse route is open, but the bank columns dominate and crop the vertical subject; rear equipment is repetitive and the pool/core loses focal clarity.
- **06_CONTROL_ROOM:** the thick foreground sill/rail and cropped console reduce the observation camera's usefulness; the separate control-interior diagnostic is stronger than this fixed view.
- **07_COMPACT_STAIR:** enclosed stair is present and compact, but the image is mostly blank shell and steps; landings, doors and route context are not visually judgeable.
- **08_MATERIAL_SLICE:** strongest material separation, but the wall, pipe and equipment surfaces are smooth; the shift-check card and labels carry more specificity than the forms.
- **09_BANK_MECHANISMS:** strong paired-bank subject coverage, but the support/upper ends are cropped; both assemblies remain close duplicates with repeated service-box language.
- **10_EAST_HIGH:** best broad room relationship after the hero, but the top bank structures crop, the pool lower read is over-bright, and the control/stair destination is visually weak.

## Diagnostic read

- **CONTROL_INTERIOR_DIAGNOSTIC:** operator use is finally visible and judgeable, but the consoles/chairs/walls are extremely clean and share a smooth manufactured response; screens and labels still carry much of the identity.
- **PLAN_DIAGNOSTIC:** centered pool, paired banks, perimeter stations and east-side annex read clearly in plan; it cannot prove scale, depth or the quality of the fixed-camera experience.
- **POOL_DEPTH_DIAGNOSTIC:** shaft depth is real and visually apparent; the water/shaft boundary is not a crisp separate read, the lower source is near-white, and rail/column occlusion hides the most important internals.

## Final handoff

Art08 should not be accepted. The strongest gains are broad hierarchy, restrained palette, visible pool depth and a now-judgeable control interior. The blocking defects are generic repeated construction, weak tactile material response, sparse/over-clean working evidence, over-prominent labels/floor graphics, unstable camera framing and an over-bright/occluded lower reactor read. No geometry, shape, coordinate or layout recipe is prescribed here; these are observable pixel defects for the parent correction cycle.
