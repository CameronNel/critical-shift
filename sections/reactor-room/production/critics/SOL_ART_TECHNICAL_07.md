# Sol technical defect audit — Art 07

## Scope

This was a read-only audit of the current newly authored `blender/reactor_scene.blend`. It was opened privately in Blender 5.2.0 LTS with `--background --factory-startup --threads 2`; no scene data was saved or changed and no old 3D file was inspected. The scene SHA-256 and `production/revisions/art-07/build_scene.py` SHA-256 both equal `a85bf2aab9129642169e3a81e14f2ea9db5b0ffa4a24da0044489f7f849c7670`.

## South service-ring scuffs — defect confirmed

The apparent raised shards in `art/renders/art-07/01_HERO.png` are not extruded shard geometry. All 19 `Route surface scuff` objects evaluate to exactly five vertices, five boundary edges, one face, no side faces, no modifiers, zero thickness, and a +Z normal. Their plan spans range from 0.070 to 0.403 m. Every vertex is at Z `0.0013000000` m while the service-ring slab top is Z `0.0000000000` m, leaving a 1.3 mm separation.

Three measured conditions produce the misleading pale-face/dark-edge reading:

- Eight pairs of scuff faces overlap each other at the exact same Z plane. Five of those pairs are on the south cluster visible to the hero camera. The coplanar intersection covers 27.0%, 31.1%, 39.4%, 56.4%, and 71.7% of the smaller south face respectively. This is exact overlapping surface geometry, not paint variation.
- Four south scuffs — `Route surface scuff`, `.003`, `.007`, and `.010` — cross the open radial seams between the segmented service-ring slabs. Those physical seams are approximately 8.2–9.2 mm wide at the scuff radii and expose a dark line directly beside or through the pale polygon.
- All scuffs remain shadow-visible in the Cycles render. The 1.3 mm air gap permits contact/cast-shadow separation under the six 480 W overhead area lights and other room lights. The low-strength world (`0.08`) makes exposed seams and occluded edges much darker than the scuff face.

The `floor_wear` material itself does not contain a black side treatment: it is opaque, non-metallic, roughness `0.90`, with only a narrow colour/roughness noise variation. Its normal input and material displacement are both unlinked. The observed black-looking sides therefore come from the coincident/scissoring floor geometry, open slab seams, and shadow/occlusion around the separated planes; they are not generated by side polygons or material displacement. Source construction: `build_scene.py:1181–1188`; material: `build_scene.py:139`; service-ring segmentation: `build_scene.py:542–560`; lighting/render setup: `build_scene.py:481–491,674,1418–1443`.

## Large closed-solid winding — defect confirmed

I converted every visible mesh to its evaluated mesh, classified closed manifold meshes with at least one world-space dimension of 0.50 m as large solids, transformed them to world space, and calculated signed volume.

- Large closed solids checked: **1,381**.
- Positive/outward signed volume: **1,365**.
- Negative/inward signed volume: **16**.
- Near-zero closed solids: **0**.

All 16 failures are the complete `Sealed riser chamfer panel` family: eight steel outer panels and eight mineral-light inner panels. Their evaluated signed volumes range from `-0.634528` to `-0.612208 m³`, confirming globally reversed winding. The source face construction is at `build_scene.py:1164–1171`.

No other evaluated large closed solid has reversed winding under this test. The one-sided roof plane, circulation arrows, and open-bottom floor patch meshes were classified as open surfaces rather than solids and were not assigned a signed-volume result.

This report records technical geometry and shading findings only. It does not assess overall visual acceptance.
