The historical isolated perch and long stair composition in the references is not layout authority and must not be copied.

## Exact visual target

The hall must read immediately as a bright, maintained 1990s industrial science-fiction power room rendered with grounded stylized semi-realism and Valorant-influenced environmental principles. It should feel manufactured, operated and serviced by adults. The simplification is deliberate, with clean silhouettes, broad value grouping and restrained surface frequency. It must look like a commercially released stylized PC environment from the gameplay cameras, not a greybox, a generic low-poly kit, a glossy sci-fi asset pack, or a photoreal product render.

The image hierarchy is fixed:

1. A deep recessed cyan reactor pool owns the centre of the frame and visibly falls into a shaft. The cyan source is concentrated below the water; a flat glowing disk fails.
2. Exactly two dominant control-bank drive assemblies rise above the pool. Their paired vertical silhouettes are the second read after the pool. They must be clearly separated, physically supported and visibly composed of fixed housing, moving carriage and drive column.
3. The south/front emergency console and pool gate provide a human-scale foreground interaction anchor. The control-room glazing on the east side sits in the rear field as a visible destination, with operator space behind it.
4. Perimeter equipment, coolant routes and workstations frame the hero without competing with it. Quiet wall areas and open circulation must remain visible.

The approved architecture governs the image: a 21.60 m maximum shell span, 16.00 m clear height, central pool, east control side at the +10 m datum, and compact stair behind the east wall immediately north of the centered east control room. The compact stair is a circulation element. It must not become a long diagonal catwalk, isolated elevated perch or room-dominating visual slash.

## Silhouette and construction language

Primary shapes must be specific enough to identify in flat grey. Use believable industrial construction: modular wall panels, real reveals, framed glazing, structural bases, service hatches, flanges, clamps, brackets, access panels, hinges and supported feet. Bevels finish a design; they cannot be the design.

The two banks are not a forest of rods and not two identical cylinders with labels pasted on. Bank A and B must have:

- fixed upper actuator housings attached to a credible overhead support;
- distinct lower moving carriages;
- two readable sliding drive columns aligned at X = -1.40 m and +1.40 m, Z = 0;
- restrained indicator/label plates and service details;
- a visible mechanical interface into the pool without hiding the pool depth;
- clean separation for the eventual vertical travel and hard-stop read.

The pool needs a substantial structural rim, 1.10 m guardrail, a single south/front 1.40 m service gate and enough visible shaft depth to feel dangerous. The stair, railings, consoles, doors and carts must have load-bearing contact and believable human proportions. Every large object needs an identifiable support, attachment or floor contact.

Use a small number of thick, legible utility routes. Coolant supply/return, sampling, local vent/relief and cable trays should enter and terminate plausibly. Utility pipes must frame the room rather than become decorative spaghetti. Console faces should communicate physical operation through grouped gauges, guarded switches, levers, breakers and early digital/CRT-like displays.

## Palette and material target

The dominant palette is restrained and functional:

- warm off-white, pale institutional grey and muted concrete for the shell and pool rim;
- desaturated teal/blue-green and blue-grey painted steel for reactor and perimeter equipment;
- charcoal/dark steel for frames, rails, bases and structural members;
- light grey or white coolant pipework with simple blue direction markings;
- cyan-blue emissive pool light as the strongest saturated hero;
- small hazard-yellow, maintenance-orange and emergency-red cues at relevant controls;
- limited utility blue/cyan indicators.

Material separation must remain visible at gameplay distance. Painted metal is matte to semi-matte with broad roughness variation and localized edge wear. Bare metal has controlled highlights and is never mirror chrome. Rubber is dark and matte. Glass is readable as framed safety glazing with reflection and thickness. Concrete/plaster has broad tonal variation. Flooring is a distinct sealed industrial surface with localized route wear. Plastics are reserved for appropriate molded parts and remain restrained; a teal material applied everywhere is an automatic failure.

Wear must be authored and localized: touched handles, floor routes, service hatches, fasteners, repaired pipe wrap and a few replacement panels. The room is maintained, not spotless and not abandoned. Do not add blanket rust, soot, grunge, edge scratches on every object, dense procedural noise or photographic texture detail.

## Lighting and exposure target

The normal state is bright and playable. Cool-white ceiling practicals provide the primary exposure. The pool adds a strong cyan secondary bounce and local glow, but it cannot bleach the walls, erase material differences or turn every surface cyan. Each practical must visibly affect nearby surfaces. Use contact shadows, occlusion, local falloff and a small amount of warm/cool separation so the machines sit in the room.

The rear and perimeter can be quieter than the hero, but nothing important may disappear into black. The east control glazing must read as glazing and an inhabited operator space, not as a black rectangle. Emission is a diegetic state cue: no decorative glowing strips, holograms or uniform bloom wash.

## Detail density and visual noise

Use three deliberate density zones:

- **Quiet:** broad wall panels, selected ceiling structure and breathing room.
- **Working:** perimeter consoles, one or two clear utility routes, door/service elements and restrained signage.
- **Focal:** pool rim/guardrail, two bank assemblies, south emergency console and a few close operator controls.

The references support physical control language: analogue gauges, segmented indicators, guarded toggles, lever handles, CRT-like screens, early flat panels, warning plates and numbered equipment. Use labels where they clarify function, especially `CONTROL BANK A`, `CONTROL BANK B`, `EMERGENCY SHUTDOWN`, `COOLANT SUPPLY`, `COOLANT RETURN` and `SAMPLING LINE`. Text must not carry the composition. If the room only reads after zooming into labels, the silhouettes failed.

Show that people work here with a small authored set of props: a clipboard or shift sheet, gloves, a maintenance tool or kit, spare component, sample container, cable/pipe repair and one personal trace. Keep these out of the 1.20 m route. Random crates, loose debris, repeated warning placards and decorative greebles are visual noise, not storytelling.

## Function and camera checks

All judgments use fixed rendered evidence from the actual gameplay/production cameras. The review set must cover entry, primary route, hero interaction, reverse view, circulation pinch point, material/lighting problem area and the dedicated elevated beauty view. Ten angles are welcome, but a flattering beauty image cannot overrule a failed gameplay view.

Every angle must make the following legible without a map overlay:

- the pool centre and depth;
- the two-bank count and paired silhouette;
- a continuous clockwise route around the pool;
- south/front emergency shutdown and its separate SCRAM, acknowledgement and bypass controls;
- east control-room glazing and its raised relationship;
- major station purpose through object form and placement;
- believable human scale, support contacts and door/stair access.

The tight turbine side has approximately 1.31 m clearance and is the danger zone. No decorative tank, cable coil, trash, structural foot or prop may narrow it below the 1.20 m dressed minimum. The compact stair sits behind the north wall per P02; do not use a long stair to fake grandeur.

## Hard acceptance score gate

Score every category independently from actual rendered pixels and measurements. The scene is not acceptable if any relevant category is below 90/100. Do not average a weak category away. A score is provisional until the relevant camera evidence exists.

| Category | 90+ means | Automatic review failure below 90 |
|---|---|---|
| Reference fidelity and art direction | Bright maintained industrial room, cyan pool hero, paired teal banks, controlled Valorant-style simplification and authored silhouettes | Reads as low-poly, generic sci-fi, dark horror, glossy asset pack, web demo or photoreal kitbash |
| Geometry construction and specificity | Primary/secondary/tertiary forms, credible supports and service logic, object-specific silhouettes | Bevelled cubes, repeated cylinders, pasted labels, primitive panels, floating/unsupported parts |
| Scale, function and circulation | Human scale, P02 envelope, east +10 m control relationship, continuous route, 1.20 m minimum and 1.31 m turbine pinch preserved | Wrong room scale, blocked door/route, giant handles, toy furniture, long isolated stair/perch, unreadable station purpose |
| Reactor hierarchy and readability | Deep pool reads first, exactly two banks read second, south emergency controls are reachable and visible | Extra dominant columns/rods, shallow glowing disk, banks merge, emergency control hidden or fused |
| Materials and anti-plastic finish | Painted metal, bare metal, rubber, glass, concrete and flooring separate in value and roughness; wear is localized | One glossy/plastic response everywhere, mirror chrome, flat untextured surfaces, procedural grime or noisy microdetail |
| Lighting, depth and exposure | Bright normal state, practical falloff, contact shadows, balanced cyan bounce, readable glazing and perimeter | Flat even exposure, cyan wash, blown emission, black secondary zones, no grounding shadows |
| Colour and detail discipline | Restrained shell, teal machinery, dark structure, white pipework, sparse hazard accents, quiet-to-focal density rhythm | Colour confetti, red room, endless warning text, decorative glow strips, every wall/asset equally busy |
| Environmental credibility and technical evidence | Operator use, plausible utility endpoints, grounded props, fixed-camera proof across all required angles and clean cold-start reproduction | Random clutter, meaningless screens, dead-room staging, missing camera evidence, failed measured checks or dependencies |

Final approval also requires zero critical defects, all cameras and objective checks passing, at least four genuine correction cycles after first visual completion, two materially stable final passes and a cold-start PASS. Technical scene structure, pivots and animation separation are judged separately from the visual score and cannot be excused by a good beauty render.

## Red flags: reject immediately and send back

Any one of these is enough to reject the pass until corrected when it is visible or materially affects gameplay:

- more than two dominant visible control-bank drives, or a visible rod forest;
- a central pool that reads as a flat cyan decal, shallow basin or opaque glowing plane;
- the historical long stair/isolated perch copied into the room, or the compact P02 stair behind the east wall north of the control room replaced by a room-dominating catwalk;
- a missing east control-room window, wrong raised relationship or a black unreadable operator space;
- less than 1.20 m usable circulation, especially at the 1.31 m turbine pinch point;
- emergency shutdown moved away from the south/front pool side, hidden behind clutter or merged with other controls;
- fixed actuator housings visually moving with the carriages, or a bank silhouette that cannot distinguish fixed, moving and sliding parts;
- objects hovering above floors, rails, brackets or consoles, or feet/pipe routes that do not meet a plausible support;
- repeated bevelled boxes pretending to be finished construction;
- most surfaces sharing a shiny teal/plastic response;
- flat lighting where every surface has the same value, or cyan emission flattening the whole room;
- blanket rust, grime, scratch noise, photographic texture frequency or abandoned-facility decay;
- screens, labels, hazard stripes or pipes used as filler after the geometry failed;
- glossy futuristic/holographic language, steampunk brass/copper language, political insignia or spaceship-corridor styling;
- a render that only works from one flattering beauty camera while route, scale, material or interaction readability fails elsewhere.
