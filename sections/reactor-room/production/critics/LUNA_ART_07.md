# Luna art spot review 07 — focus views

Bounded review of the supplied Art 07 focus renders only: `01_HERO.png`, `06_CONTROL_ROOM.png`, `08_MATERIAL_SLICE.png`, `09_BANK_MECHANISMS.png` and `CONTROL_INTERIOR_DIAGNOSTIC.png`. No full-room rubric or acceptance score is issued.

## Glass and control-space status

The prior control-glass failure is substantially resolved in the supplied pixels.

- `06_CONTROL_ROOM.png` now gives a clear view through the observation glazing. The pool, floor route, perimeter machines and bank column are readable without the previous broad doubled reflection wash.
- The opaque lower horizontal sill/spandrel remains visible as modeled structure and does not read as a glazing/refraction error.
- `CONTROL_INTERIOR_DIAGNOSTIC.png` no longer has the previous featureless black occluder consuming half the frame. Two operator desks, chairs, gauges, physical switches, screens, mug, paperwork and the view back into the hall are all visible.
- Some ordinary fixture/glass reflections remain, but they no longer prevent the room from being judged. I do not see the prior severe opaque or inverted-glass presentation in these focus images.

The control interior is now evidence of a human-operated room, with a stronger functional read than Art 06. Its consoles still look simplified and clean, and the visible screens/labels carry a large share of the specificity.

## View-specific observations

### 01 HERO

The pool and two-bank hierarchy remain strong. The bank housings now have more visible service language: vents, side modules, fasteners, handles and small status elements. The east glazing reads cleanly. The pale wall and floor shell is covered by large repeated soft contour shapes that are visible across the background panels and around the pool; these patterns are too regular and too widely distributed to read as restrained wear. They pull the room toward procedural texture noise.

### 06 CONTROL_ROOM

The perspective observation view is now useful. The central pool and perimeter stations are visible through the glass, and the sill reads as a solid architectural element. The glass still has a faint reflective layer, but it is no longer the dominant problem. The pale shell’s repeated contour pattern is visible through the glazing as well, weakening the clean industrial read.

### 08 MATERIAL_SLICE

The close-up has good subject coverage: white service pipes with flanges, yellow wheels, a teal tank, dark base, pressure gauge and shift-check notice all read separately. The bank of pipe fittings and gauge are more specific than earlier passes. The large contour/splotch pattern covers the pale wall and floor around the equipment and is also visible on the pale pipe/adjacent surfaces. It reads as an all-over procedural overlay, not low-frequency material variation or localized use. The pipes and wall still have a smooth, manufactured plastic-like response in the highlights.

### 09 BANK_MECHANISMS

The two banks are clear, and the service faces have more visual information than Art 06: side modules, vents, status blocks, handles, fasteners and lower trim are visible. The assemblies are still close visual cousins with repeated cylindrical shells, matching trim rings and similarly proportioned service doors. Their construction remains generic when compared with the more authored industrial specificity in A02/B01. The repeated pale contour pattern is especially obvious across the background shell and ceiling, adding noise around the focal mechanisms.

### CONTROL_INTERIOR_DIAGNOSTIC

This is a useful correction. The black occluder is gone, the desks and chairs are fully visible, and the room shows operator traces through a mug, paperwork, physical switches, gauges and status screens. The view back into the hall is readable through the glazing. The interior is still visually sparse and very clean, and the pale wall pattern repeats across the whole room. The consoles read as stylized equipment, but their screens and labels are generic compared with the grounded physical-control language in B01.

## Remaining observable gaps

- The repeated contour/splotch pattern across pale shell, floor and control-room surfaces is the dominant Art 07 defect. It is visible in every supplied focus view and reads as procedural noise.
- The pattern is too broad and too regular to communicate authored wear or believable material breakup.
- Bank service faces are more detailed but still generic and closely duplicated between A and B.
- White pipework, pale wall panels and flooring remain smooth and have weak material separation in the close material view.
- The control interior is now judgeable, but it remains sparse and label/screen-dependent for identity.
- The references retain quiet surfaces with selective wear and stronger tactile painted-metal separation; these focus renders still look more procedurally treated and less authored.

## Bounded decision

The glass ghosting and diagnostic occlusion problems are materially improved and no longer block this focus review. Art 07 still has a clear visual regression in the repeated pale contour texture, plus unresolved generic bank service construction and smooth material response. A full-room acceptance decision remains out of scope for this partial batch.
