# Spawn Room Lighting Reference

**Primary visual plate:** [11_lighting_plan.svg](reference/11_lighting_plan.svg)  
**Camera plate:** [15_camera_validation.svg](reference/15_camera_validation.svg)

Lighting is part of the style proof. It must be solved in the validation slice before room expansion.

## 1. Hallway

Intent:
- safe;
- institutional;
- readable;
- slightly oppressive.

Use:
- neutral/warm fluorescent-style practicals;
- visible fixture bodies;
- moderate falloff;
- darker ceiling/service recesses;
- operations threshold can shift cooler.

Avoid:
- every surface at the same value;
- broad ambient light that erases shadows;
- permanent cinematic orange/teal grade.

## 2. Briefing room

Intent:
- calm optional social space;
- tutorial wall visible from entry.

Use:
- one dominant practical group;
- modest wall wash/focal emphasis at briefing screen;
- screen emission kept local;
- readable chairs with soft contact shadow.

Avoid:
- screen as primary room light;
- theater darkness;
- RGB accent lighting.

## 3. Locker / suiting room

Intent:
- ritualized preparation;
- PPE is visually important;
- central chamber is functional, not sacred.

Use:
- broad room practicals;
- restrained vertical/upper light in suit bays;
- enough shadow inside bays to give depth;
- chamber indicator lighting only during state changes;
- contact shadow under boots, bench, chamber and bay structure.

Avoid:
- retail display lighting around each suit;
- bright glowing chamber frame;
- flat white light filling every recess.

## 4. Operations threshold

Intent:
- clear transition from safe preparation to dangerous plant.

Use:
- slightly cooler/harder light;
- stronger contrast around heavy door;
- radiation checkpoint readable but secondary.

This transition should be felt before it is explained by text.

## 5. Exposure hierarchy

From the spawn entry:
1. operational exit / major route remains readable;
2. locker hero cluster is inviting;
3. briefing room is visible but optional;
4. quiet walls remain quiet.

Do not make a poster or small wall device the brightest thing in frame.

## 6. Contact and grounding

Every support-dependent prop must be checked for:
- local shadow;
- visible support/mount;
- believable occlusion;
- no 1–5 cm “floating” gap.

The geometric support validator and the lighting review are separate gates. Pass both.

## 7. Lighting diagnostic render

Use a grayscale/value review:
- can you identify the focal zones without color?
- are corners allowed to be darker?
- do all props have contact?
- is any material highlight unnaturally bright?
- does the floor read as a surface rather than a mirror?

If no, fix lighting before adding more props.
