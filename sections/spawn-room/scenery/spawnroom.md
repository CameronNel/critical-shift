# 1. Purpose

The Spawn Room is the player's first physical experience of Critical Shift. It must establish the game's visual identity, the scale of the facility, the social co-op tone, the safety ritual, and the feeling that ordinary humans are working inside an enormous AI-run industrial complex.

The layout itself is deliberately simple. The quality must come from composition, stylized architecture, strong silhouettes, careful materials, lighting, environmental storytelling and deliberate prop dressing rather than convoluted geometry.

The playable space is only a small slice of a much larger building.

Players spawn in a short internal hallway. A briefing room is on the left. A locker and suit-preparation room is on the right. The operational exit is straight ahead at the end of the short hallway. A Geiger/radiation checkpoint sits beside that exit.

The player should immediately understand the space without a map.

At first glance:

- left = optional briefing/tutorial
- right = suit-up and integrity check
- forward = production facility and danger

Nothing about the composition should obscure this basic reading.

---

# 2. Non-Negotiable Layout

## 2.1 Central hallway

The spawn point sits within a short corridor inside a much larger administration/preparation building.

Target dimensions are guidance, not immutable gameplay law until the section is validated in engine:

- clear hallway width: approximately 3.0–3.4 m
- playable hallway length from spawn zone to operational exit: approximately 8–10 m
- ceiling height: approximately 3.2–3.8 m
- interior room doors: approximately 1.2–1.4 m wide
- operational exit: approximately 1.5–1.8 m wide and visually heavier

Four players must be able to gather, turn, gesture, carry small equipment and pass each other without the hallway feeling like a hotel corridor.

## 2.2 Left room: briefing room

The briefing room opens from the left side of the hallway.

It is optional. Players who already understand the game should be able to ignore it completely.

Players who enter should immediately understand that they can sit down with friends and watch the tutorial/shift briefing.

Target usable footprint: roughly 5–6 m wide by 4.5–5.5 m deep.

## 2.3 Right room: locker room

The locker room opens from the right side of the hallway.

It is the visual hero area of the spawn cluster.

The fixed composition is:

- two complete suit stations along the left side
- two complete suit stations along the right side
- one integrity-check chamber occupying the central axis
- comfortable circulation around the integrity chamber
- clear return path to the hallway

Exactly four primary suits are presented.

## 2.4 Operational exit

The exit into the dangerous facility is straight ahead at the end of the hallway.

The Geiger/radiation checkpoint is placed directly beside or immediately before the exit so the object reads as the final safety threshold.

# 3. Larger-Building Illusion

The playable hallway must feel embedded within a much larger structure.

Do not solve this by building enormous accessible empty rooms.

Use controlled visual evidence:

- thick walls and deep door reveals
- locked or non-playable personnel/service doors
- signage pointing toward unseen departments
- overhead ducts and pipes continuing through walls
- cable trays disappearing beyond the playable architecture
- inaccessible corridor glimpses through wired-glass panes or narrow windows
- distant industrial ambience
- muffled public-address announcements
- vibration or low mechanical hum through walls
- numbering conventions suggesting many other rooms
- fire doors, service hatches and utility risers
- ceiling infrastructure that clearly continues beyond the section
- occasional glimpses of an atrium, stairwell or service corridor through glass without allowing traversal

The result should make the player think: “We occupy one preparation corridor inside a huge institution.”

Do not make fake space so visually interesting that players constantly attempt to access it. Locked areas should support scale, not frustrate navigation.

---

# 4. Visual Style

This room follows the global art direction.

PEAK is a high-level reference for simplicity, readability, stylized proportions, approachable forms, broad colour blocking and the ability to make low-detail geometry feel authored.

Do not copy PEAK-specific assets, characters, rooms, props, costumes, logos or distinctive designs.

The Spawn Room must be:

- stylized
- cleanly modeled
- low-to-mid poly
- deliberately faceted where attractive
- soft and graphic in material treatment
- readable at gameplay distance
- charming without becoming childish
- industrial without becoming gritty photorealism
- detailed through authored composition rather than micro-geometry

It must not become:

- photorealistic AAA
- generic sci-fi
- a web-graphics demo
- shiny plastic
- primitive boxes with materials slapped on
- an empty greybox decorated with random props
- a dense kitbash of realistic assets
- a grunge-covered abandoned facility
- a glossy showroom
- a toy room

The scene should feel handmade even when it is procedurally or agent-generated.

# 5. Architectural Language

## 5.1 Walls

Use large modular wall fields rather than flat uninterrupted boxes.

Possible construction:

- broad painted industrial wall panels
- a stronger lower-wall zone or tiled dado
- chunky skirting/base pieces
- occasional broad vertical ribs
- inset access panels
- deep frames around doors
- a few visible fastening points only where they help rhythm
- restrained service conduits

Avoid fields of tiny screws, panels, seams and technical greebles.

Wall thickness must be visually believable. Doors should not look cut into paper-thin planes.

## 5.2 Ceiling

The ceiling should establish building rhythm.

Use:

- repeated recessed practical lights
- one or two broad ventilation ducts
- occasional acoustic/service panels
- cable or utility run where composition benefits
- sprinkler or safety fixtures simplified into strong shapes
- one ceiling access hatch
- selective exposed structure near the operational threshold

Do not hang dozens of pipes and cables overhead.

## 5.3 Floor

The floor should be visually richer than a single uninterrupted material.

Preferred language:

- large sealed industrial tiles or robust institutional tiles
- restrained variation between tile units
- localized replacement tiles
- subtle wear on primary foot routes
- rubber threshold strips
- a few inset utility covers
- a small drainage or service detail near the integrity chamber if functional

One or two damaged or replaced tiles are welcome.

Do not cover the whole floor in cracks or dirt.

# 6. Central Hallway Dressing

The hallway should feel occupied and maintained by humans.

## 6.1 Spawn side

The initial spawn area should remain relatively uncluttered so four players can appear together without intersecting props.

Use visual framing rather than furniture around the exact spawn points.

Possible elements around the spawn end:

- a facility-section identifier
- clock or shift-status panel
- modest bench pushed against one wall
- wall-mounted notice board
- one medium plant
- framed worker portrait or institutional artwork
- sealed utility cabinet
- waste/recycling bin
- directional sign cluster

## 6.2 Wall information

The hallway should contain a small number of specific, readable document groupings instead of wallpaper made from random papers.

Suggested subjects:

- CURRENT SHIFT ROSTER
- PRE-ENTRY SAFETY CHECK
- FACILITY MAP — STAFF OVERVIEW
- WORKER OF THE CYCLE
- REPORT ALL SEAL DAMAGE
- RADIATION CONTROL AREA BEYOND THIS POINT
- TODAY'S MAINTENANCE NOTICES
- HUMAN CONTRIBUTION PROGRAMME poster
- emergency muster route

Use large graphic hierarchy. At normal walking distance, players should recognize that each cluster is a meaningful category even if small body text is decorative.

## 6.3 Portraits

Include 2–4 framed portraits across the spawn cluster, not necessarily all in the hallway.

They can represent:

- a respected long-serving worker
- a safety supervisor
- a historic facility commissioning crew
- an exemplary shift team

Portraits should feel institutional but human.

Use simple frames with matte finishes, modest plaques and stylized adult faces.

Do not create rows of identical corporate headshots.

## 6.4 Plants

Plants are deliberate human softness inside machine architecture.

Hallway target:

- one medium floor plant
- optionally one small wall/shelf plant

Shape language:

- broad stylized leaves
- clean silhouette
- matte ceramic or molded pot
- no photoreal foliage cards
- no jungle density

A slightly imperfect plant, one leaning stem or one browned leaf can make the environment feel inhabited without requiring dirt everywhere.

## 6.5 Chairs and bench

A short waiting bench or 2–3 linked chairs may sit against one wall.

They must not narrow the main route.

Use chunky institutional furniture:

- broad seat/back shapes
- simple metal or molded frame
- restrained padded material
- subtle wear at edges
- legs thick enough to read
- no flimsy realistic office-chair geometry

One seat may be slightly misaligned or carry a clipboard to break sterility.

# 7. Briefing Room

## 7.1 Functional intent

The left room is a voluntary social/tutorial room.

It should communicate:

“Sit down together and watch the briefing if you want it.”

It should not feel mandatory.

The doorway should expose enough of the room to reveal seats and the tutorial display from the hallway.

## 7.2 Layout

Preferred arrangement:

- tutorial display on the wall opposite or slightly angled from the entrance
- 4 primary usable chairs facing the display
- up to 2 additional spare chairs
- clear aisle around seating
- small side cabinet/table
- notice/art wall
- modest plant
- no central conference table blocking the view

Seats should be grouped socially, not in military-perfect rows.

Slight variations help:

- one chair moved a little
- one spare chair stacked or placed against wall
- one clipboard resting on a seat
- one cup or paper packet on the side cabinet

Keep these details sparse enough to remain readable.

## 7.3 Tutorial focal wall

The focal wall should be a designed composition.

Include:

- large industrial screen or protected monitor
- chunky housing or mounting frame
- simple playback/status controls
- title panel such as SHIFT BRIEFING / PRE-ENTRY ORIENTATION
- small speakers or integrated grille
- nearby safety icon strip
- soft wall wash light or focused practical

The screen must not resemble a modern luxury TV simply attached to a wall.

It belongs to the facility.

## 7.4 Briefing room wall dressing

Use a combination of:

### Official material
- safety procedure poster
- emergency muster sheet
- suit integrity diagram
- radiation basics
- shift quota information
- equipment return checklist

### Human material
- crew photo
- employee recognition sheet
- handwritten reminder
- pinned thank-you card
- small birthday/event notice
- old training completion photo

### Art/morale
- original stylized poster celebrating human infrastructure work
- graphic industrial landscape print
- historical facility commissioning image

The facility administration can be gently propagandistic. It should feel believable and slightly funny because of context, not because every poster contains a joke.

## 7.5 Furniture materials

Chairs:

- matte tubular or chunky molded frame
- muted vinyl/fabric seat
- broad stylized shapes
- no cloth weave
- no shiny polypropylene look

Cabinet/table:

- powder-coated metal or laminate
- soft roughness
- clear silhouette
- limited edge wear

## 7.6 Plants

One medium or small plant is enough.

A briefing room plant should imply that someone consciously tried to make a sterile workplace nicer.

# 8. Locker Room

## 8.1 Functional intent

The locker room is the signature preparation space.

It should make players want to walk in and inspect their suit.

The room must instantly read as organized around four worker positions and a central safety machine.

## 8.2 Overall composition

When viewed from the doorway:

- two suit bays are visible on the left
- two suit bays are visible on the right
- the integrity chamber anchors the center
- the far wall provides restrained supporting detail
- circulation remains obvious around the chamber

Target room footprint:

- width: approximately 6–7 m
- depth: approximately 5.5–6.5 m
- ceiling height: approximately 3.6–4.2 m

Exact dimensions may change after first-person validation, but the symmetry/readability must remain.

## 8.3 Suit stations

There are exactly four primary suit stations.

Each suit bay should be a coherent mini-composition rather than a suit floating in front of a locker.

Each station may contain:

- one mounted/hanging suit
- one helmet cradle or shelf
- gloves
- boots or boot dock
- life-support/filter/telemetry pack
- player or station identifier
- status indicator
- equipment hooks
- storage cubby/locker
- personal card/photo area
- a small checklist panel
- floor mat or standing zone
- restrained color identity

The four bays should share a modular design system while having small human differences.

Examples of controlled variation:

- different pinned note
- different mug/photo
- slightly different helmet orientation
- one spare glove
- different player colour identifier
- one maintenance tag

Do not make one bay pristine and another destroyed.

## 8.4 Hero suit presentation

The suit must be one of the best-looking objects in the room.

Presentation requirements:

- full silhouette readable from entrance
- compact stylized proportions
- oversized but not childish helmet/visor
- clear torso mass
- thick readable gloves
- sturdy boots
- simple life-support pack
- rescue/grab handle
- broad colour panels
- very limited small hardware
- visible seal/closure area
- dosimeter/status module

The suit should feel substantial but soft enough to be worn.

Avoid:

- glossy astronaut plastic
- military tactical clutter
- realistic fabric folds everywhere
- tiny straps
- mannequin stiffness
- toy-like proportions
- generic sci-fi armor

Use lighting to separate each suit from its bay without turning the stations into retail displays.

## 8.5 Suit bay architecture

Suit bays can use:

- shallow recessed niche
- chunky upper shelf
- strong vertical frame
- locker/storage module
- broad back panel in controlled colour
- simple bay light
- floor mat
- label panel

They should not simply be four rectangular cupboards.

Introduce gentle tapering, softened corners, asymmetry in support brackets or other stylized shape choices where useful.

# 9. Integrity Check Chamber

## 9.1 Role

The central chamber is the hero machine of the room.

Its purpose is to inspect suit integrity before workers enter the hazardous plant.

The chamber should communicate:

- step inside
- close/seal
- scan/pressurize/test
- receive pass/fail state
- exit

It must be readable without requiring a long UI explanation.

## 9.2 Form

Preferred form:

- compact upright chamber
- broad frame
- partial transparent enclosure
- clear entry opening/door
- floor platform
- overhead scanner or seal assembly
- chunky side equipment
- status display
- large readable lights
- obvious handle or door mechanism

Target footprint:

- roughly 1.8–2.2 m wide
- roughly 1.8–2.2 m deep
- roughly 2.6–3.0 m high

Keep adequate space around it.

## 9.3 Materials

Use:

- painted/powder-coated body
- dark rubber seals
- modest glass/acrylic panel
- restrained metallic mechanical pieces
- broad indicator lights
- matte floor plate

Do not make the chamber a transparent shiny sci-fi tube.

## 9.4 States

Design visual hooks for:

- idle
- occupied
- scanning
- pass
- integrity concern
- fail/emergency

State changes should use:

- light pattern
- simple display
- mechanical movement
- short sound cue
- perhaps a physical indicator arm/gauge

Colour must not be the only signal.

# 10. Locker Room Supporting Dressing

## 10.1 Benches

Provide one or two short benches where they do not obstruct suit access.

Bench qualities:

- chunky industrial frame
- broad seat
- slightly softened edges
- matte surface
- subtle local scuffing

## 10.2 Technical posters

Required themes:

- SUIT SEAL SEQUENCE
- INTEGRITY FAILURE — DO NOT EXIT
- DOSIMETER REFERENCE
- CONTAMINATION RESPONSE
- HELMET LOCK CHECK
- FILTER / PACK CONNECTION
- BEFORE YOU LEAVE

Use large icons and simplified diagrams.

## 10.3 Personal material

A few suit bays may contain:

- small family/friend photo
- handwritten note
- shift-sticker
- tiny charm
- old recognition badge
- folded paper

Keep personal material tasteful and readable.

## 10.4 Maintenance evidence

Add small signs of active maintenance:

- inspection tag
- replacement filter box
- one compact tool tray
- maintenance log
- removable floor/access plate
- neatly coiled hose or cable

Avoid maintenance clutter spilling through the room.

## 10.5 Floor treatment

Use:

- durable tile/sealed surface
- rubber standing mats in front of suit bays
- a visually distinct zone around integrity chamber
- subtle drainage/service seam if needed
- 1–2 replaced tiles or repair patches

# 11. Operational Exit

## 11.1 Door

The exit should feel heavier than the briefing and locker doors.

Use:

- thick frame
- broad slab/leaf
- visible seal
- chunky handle or powered opening mechanism
- small protected viewport if useful
- access/status panel
- restrained hazard marking
- overhead identification sign

Suggested wording:

- CONTROLLED AREA
- OPERATIONS ACCESS
- PPE REQUIRED BEYOND THIS POINT

The door should create a clear visual threshold between safe preparation and production.

## 11.2 Atmosphere

The immediate area around the exit may have:

- slightly cooler or harsher light
- stronger machine hum
- more visible service infrastructure
- subtle airflow/fog beyond the opening when active
- a glimpse of a more industrial corridor on the other side

Do not make the safe side suddenly horror-dark.

---

# 12. Geiger / Radiation Check Station

## 12.1 Placement

Place it at the exit, either:

- wall-mounted immediately beside the exit, or
- as a compact standing checkpoint immediately before the door

It must never be mistaken for a random electrical panel.

## 12.2 Form

Possible components:

- large readable meter/display
- physical probe or integrated detector
- chunky speaker/grille
- radiation icon
- simple instruction plate
- pass/caution indicator
- calibration/status light
- modest protective housing

A partial arch or scan frame may be used only if it remains simple and does not compete with the integrity chamber.

## 12.3 Style

The unit should feel like mature facility hardware:

- somewhat old
- maintained
- simple
- sturdy
- legible
- slightly charming in its physicality

Not a sleek touchscreen kiosk.

---

# 13. Papers, Posters and Portrait Production

Paper content should be designed as actual visual assets, not random grey rectangles.

Use categories with distinct composition.

## 13.1 Formal sheets

- headers
- diagrams
- boxed warnings
- signatures/stamps
- checkboxes
- a few highlighted lines
- clean margins

## 13.2 Informal sheets

- handwritten notes
- marker circles
- tape
- pinning
- curled corner
- slight rotation

## 13.3 Mounting

Papers must visually exist in the world.

Use:

- thin paper planes/cards
- cork/magnetic board
- clips
- tape
- simple frames
- clear protective sleeves where appropriate

Do not leave images hovering a millimeter off the wall.

## 13.4 Portrait quality

Portrait art should match the game's stylization.

Avoid:

- uncanny photoreal faces
- random stock photography
- direct copies of real people
- inconsistent art styles

---

# 14. Plants

Plants are accent props, not environmental filler.

Target total across the whole spawn section: approximately 2–4 meaningful plants.

Use different scale categories:

- one medium floor plant
- one smaller briefing-room plant
- optional tiny desk/shelf plant

Make their silhouettes strong enough to read.

Avoid thin alpha-card foliage and default Blender-looking plastic leaves.

---

# 15. Rubble, Damage and Wear

The spawn area is functioning.

Damage is limited.

Acceptable:

- one small corner with tile fragments after a repair
- a chipped skirting section
- one patched wall panel
- a few floor scuffs
- subtle wear around handles
- minor chair scratches
- one cable cover replacement
- one imperfect tile

Unacceptable:

- collapse debris
- piles of rubble
- grime everywhere
- rusty abandonment
- broken ceiling
- smashed furniture
- horror set dressing

A clean environment that has a few specific histories is more believable than generic dirt.

---

# 16. Material Direction

The plastic-looking failure mode must be actively avoided.

Material families:

1. matte/off-matte painted wall material
2. slightly warmer or cooler tile family
3. powder-coated metal
4. muted structural metal
5. dark rubber
6. soft vinyl/padded chair surface
7. matte paper/card
8. modest glass
9. plant foliage
10. soil and matte pot
11. restrained emissive indicators
12. screen glass

Most objects should use moderate-to-high roughness.

Vary roughness intentionally by material family.

Do not simply reduce gloss globally and call it finished. Material identity must come from colour, roughness, value, shape, lighting and selective surface variation.

Avoid:

- default Principled BSDF look
- uniform plastic roughness
- chrome-like trims
- wet-looking floors
- noisy photoreal normal maps
- texture packs pasted onto stylized geometry

---

# 17. Lighting

## 17.1 Hallway

The hallway is safe, welcoming and readable.

Use:

- clear overhead practical rhythm
- mild warm/cool variation where helpful
- enough ambient fill to read faces and props
- stronger visual pull toward the exit
- soft accents around art/notice clusters

## 17.2 Briefing room

Slightly warmer and softer.

The tutorial display is the focal point, but the room must not become dark during idle state.

## 17.3 Locker room

Brightest and cleanest sub-room.

Provide good separation on all four suit silhouettes.

The integrity chamber should have subtle dedicated lighting.

## 17.4 Exit

Slight increase in contrast and industrial character.

The Geiger station and operational door should be immediately visible.

Do not flood the area red or orange unless an actual alarm state exists.

---

# 18. Audio Hooks

Although this is a scenery specification, environment construction should leave space for:

- distant ventilation
- low transformer/mechanical hum
- subtle PA announcements
- chair movement
- tutorial screen audio
- locker equipment clicks
- suit-status beeps
- integrity chamber seal/scan sounds
- Geiger clicks near the exit
- heavier machinery ambience beyond the operational door

The safe zone should feel alive, not silent.

# 19. Composition Cameras for Validation

At minimum validate the section from these views:

## Camera A — Spawn view

From the initial player position looking forward.

Must show:

- left briefing entrance
- right locker entrance
- exit ahead

## Camera B — Locker doorway

Must clearly show:

- two suit stations left
- two suit stations right
- integrity chamber central

## Camera C — Briefing-room entrance

Must clearly show:

- seating
- tutorial display
- usable aisle

## Camera D — Exit-facing reverse view

From near the operational exit looking back.

Must show that the room remains coherent from the opposite direction.

## Camera E — Human-height circulation views

Walk every edge of the room at first-person camera height and inspect clipping, unreadable corners, over-scaled furniture and awkward blank surfaces.

Beauty shots alone are insufficient.

---

# 20. Prop Density Hierarchy

Every view should have a clear hierarchy:

1. architecture and route
2. hero function
3. secondary composition
4. narrative dressing
5. micro accents

If a player notices tiny paper clutter before understanding the locker chamber, the hierarchy is wrong.

If every wall is empty, the room is under-dressed.

If every wall has art, notes, pipes, switches and props, the room is over-dressed.

Negative space is intentional.

---

# 21. Acceptance Checklist

The Spawn Room is not complete until all are true.

## Layout
- short central hallway is immediately legible
- optional briefing room is left
- locker room is right
- operational exit is forward
- no major prop blocks four-player movement

## Briefing room
- seating for four is clearly usable
- tutorial focal display is obvious
- room feels optional and social
- walls have controlled human and institutional detail

## Locker room
- exactly two hero suits on left
- exactly two hero suits on right
- integrity check chamber is central
- each suit station feels authored
- room is easy to circulate

## Exit
- operational door reads as an important threshold
- Geiger/radiation station is directly associated with the exit
- stronger safety language is visible

## Building scale
- player perceives a much larger building beyond the playable slice
- inaccessible architecture supports scale without creating route confusion

## Art
- stylized, not photoreal
- low-to-mid poly but not primitive
- no random blockout-quality forms
- no plastic default-material appearance
- no generic sci-fi kitbash
- materials are tactile and graphic
- lighting is atmospheric but readable

## Dressing
- portraits exist and are integrated
- meaningful information papers exist
- original wall art/morale graphics exist
- plants are used sparingly
- chairs/benches are polished
- localized rubble/repair evidence exists
- tile variation exists
- clutter never obscures gameplay

## Final emotional read

The area should feel like a real, slightly strange workplace where four human friends prepare for a dangerous shift inside an enormous machine-run facility.

That feeling matters more than adding another thousand polygons.
