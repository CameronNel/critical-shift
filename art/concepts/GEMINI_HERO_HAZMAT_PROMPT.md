# Gemini Prompt: Critical Shift Hero Hazmat Suit

## Purpose

Use this prompt to generate the **signature player hazmat suit** for Critical Shift. The output is a visual design reference for a shippable 3D game character, not a photoreal illustration and not a direct copy of another game's character.

## Prompt

Create a **hero character concept sheet for the signature hazmat worker suit in the stylized co-op game CRITICAL SHIFT**.

The game is a darkly funny cooperative industrial game about human workers operating a uranium mine, fuel refinery, and nuclear power plant. The character must feel small, brave, practical, slightly awkward, and visually memorable inside enormous dangerous machinery.

### Overall visual direction

Use the **high-level degree of stylization, simplicity, readability, and low-detail 3D appeal associated with PEAK as the visual north star**, while creating a completely original Critical Shift design. Do **not** copy PEAK scouts, costumes, faces, badges, backpacks, props, logos, colour layouts, or other distinctive assets.

Target qualities:

- Stylized 3D game-character concept, not realism.
- Simple low-poly / low-to-mid-poly forms with intentional soft faceting.
- Compact adult worker proportions, roughly 5.5–6.5 heads tall.
- Slightly enlarged helmet/head volume.
- Simplified arms and legs.
- Chunky readable gloves and boots.
- Strong silhouette readable from far away.
- Broad uninterrupted colour blocks.
- Very little texture noise.
- Friendly, expressive shape language contrasted with genuinely hazardous industrial equipment.
- Feels designed for physics, ragdolls, carrying, dragging, climbing, grabbing valves, and operating big machinery.

### Signature suit design

Design one **iconic sealed industrial hazmat suit** that can become the game's default hero outfit.

Include:

- A distinctive oversized sealed hood/helmet shape integrated into the suit.
- A large practical visor opening that can show a simple stylized human face when lighting permits.
- A thick visible neck/helmet seal.
- A compact torso with one clear central suit-seal or closure mechanism.
- Chunky protective gloves with simplified fingers or mitten-like segmented forms suitable for first-person interaction.
- Heavy simplified safety boots.
- A compact backpack combining telemetry, filtration, cooling, and life-support functions into one strong shape.
- One large rescue/grab handle on the upper back, obvious enough for another player to drag the wearer by it.
- One or two thick readable hoses at most. Avoid spaghetti tubing.
- A large dosimeter/status module on the chest, shoulder, belt, or pack with simple emissive state lights.
- A few large seam/panel breaks only where they help the silhouette or explain how the suit seals.
- Simple department/player colour panels that could be recoloured for four players.
- Small original industrial identification markings, but no existing real-world company logos and no copied game logos.
- Optional compact belt/tool clips, kept extremely simple.

### Critical Shift personality

The suit should feel:

- Industrial rather than military.
- Protective rather than tactical.
- Slightly cumbersome but lovable.
- Built by a machine-run bureaucracy that values standardisation and telemetry.
- Plausibly mass-produced in large numbers.
- Easy to identify when crumpled in a ragdoll pose.
- Funny in motion without looking like a mascot costume.

The design should be visually strong even if all tiny details are removed.

### Colour direction

Show the hero version primarily in a **high-visibility industrial base colour** such as safety yellow, warm orange, or another strong hazard colour, contrasted with dark charcoal gloves/boots/harness pieces and a restrained cool emissive status accent.

Keep the palette to a few large colour families. Do not scatter many tiny coloured details across the suit.

If showing player variants, recolour only intentional panels/bands/modules while keeping the base suit language identical.

### Material direction

Materials must be simple and game-feasible:

- Matte or softly rough suit body.
- Simple dark rubber-like gloves and boots.
- Simple semi-gloss visor.
- Minimal hard-plastic/painted-metal pack parts.
- Sparse emissive indicators.
- No realistic cloth weave.
- No skin pores.
- No tiny scratches.
- No dense edge wear.
- No photoreal PBR texture pass.
- No painterly brush texture treatment.

### Concept-sheet composition

Present the design on a clean neutral background as a professional game-development model sheet.

Preferred layout:

1. Large three-quarter hero view.
2. Front view.
3. Side view.
4. Back view clearly showing the pack and rescue handle.
5. Small close-up of helmet/visor and suit seal.
6. Optional small silhouette showing the character next to an oversized industrial valve or control console for scale.

Keep all views consistent as the same suit design.

### Gameplay-state design

If space permits, include tiny secondary thumbnails for:

- Unsealed / preparation state.
- Fully sealed state.
- Damaged suit state with one large readable breach indicator.
- Contaminated state using a broad graphic contamination treatment.
- Emergency/low-integrity state using clear emissive warning changes.

These should be state variations of the same model, not separate costumes.

### Explicit negatives

Do not create:

- Photorealism.
- Grounded realistic character rendering.
- Realistic military or tactical operator gear.
- Tactical vests, weapon pouches, MOLLE clutter, or soldier aesthetics.
- Sea of Thieves-style painterly textures or pirate-like exaggeration.
- Hyper-detailed hard-surface armour.
- Dense greebles.
- Hundreds of stitches, seams, buckles, clips, or fasteners.
- Realistic fabric wrinkles everywhere.
- Generic NASA astronaut styling.
- Generic modern firefighter styling.
- A direct copy of a PEAK scout or any other existing game character.
- Child/toddler coding or baby-like proportions.
- Chibi/anime proportions.
- Mascot/plush-toy materials.
- Weapons.

### Final test

The suit succeeds if its silhouette is recognisable in one second, looks appealing with very little detail, can plausibly be built as a modest-poly 3D game asset, clearly belongs in a dangerous radioactive workplace, and feels original to Critical Shift.

Render this as a **shippable stylized 3D game character design reference**, not as cinematic key art.

## Follow-up Iteration Prompt

After Gemini produces a promising design, use this for refinement:

> Keep the exact same core suit design and silhouette. Simplify it by another 20–30%: remove tiny seams, clips, surface panels, realistic folds, texture noise, and unnecessary hoses. Make the helmet, gloves, boots, central seal, backpack, rescue handle, and dosimeter more readable from gameplay distance. Strengthen broad colour blocking and preserve the compact stylized proportions. The result should look cheaper and cleaner to model while becoming more iconic, not less detailed merely for its own sake.
