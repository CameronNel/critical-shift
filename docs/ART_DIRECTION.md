# Art Direction

## Core Direction

Critical Shift uses **production-feasible stylised industrial science fiction** with adult human characters, simplified modular machinery, readable silhouettes, and strong environmental storytelling.

The target is a deliberate **low-to-mid fidelity co-op game**, not photorealism and not AAA industrial simulation. The visual identity should come from shape language, colour, lighting, animation, sound, and environmental storytelling rather than dense geometry or expensive surface detail.

Characters must retain believable adult anatomy. Stylisation may simplify surfaces, amplify workwear, and strengthen silhouettes, but must not use oversized childlike heads, tiny torsos, stubby limbs, mascot faces, glossy toy materials, or nursery-like proportions.

Physical comedy comes from responsive animation, ragdolls, collisions, awkward teamwork, heavy equipment, rushed decisions, and the contrast between ordinary workers and enormous industrial systems. It must not depend on infantilised character design or high-end animation fidelity.

## Production Target

The project is built by one developer with extensive AI assistance. Visual design must therefore favour assets that agents can generate, assemble, reuse, validate, and optimise reliably.

Preferred production qualities:

- Low-to-mid polygon counts with clean, broad forms.
- Modular walls, floors, pipes, rails, platforms, supports, doors, and machine shells.
- Repeated industrial kits rather than unique architecture everywhere.
- Limited shared material families and trim sheets.
- Flat or restrained physically based materials with modest roughness variation.
- Small reusable grime, hazard, leak, scorch, and contamination decals.
- Lighting, fog, alarm states, emissive panels, and moving machinery providing atmosphere.
- Large readable controls and interaction points.
- Simplified pipe networks and silhouettes rather than engineering-detail clutter.
- Controlled prop density with clear gameplay paths.
- Reusable machine cores dressed differently for each department.
- Strong level-of-detail discipline and object-count limits.

A finished scene should look intentional and atmospheric at gameplay distance while remaining obviously achievable by a small team. Screenshots may be moody and striking, but must not imply film-quality assets, photogrammetry, dense micro-geometry, unique texture work on every surface, or hundreds of bespoke props.

## Environment

The mine, refinery, reactor, turbine systems, medical area, locker rooms, compliance spaces, and transport infrastructure belong to one connected industrial site. The facility should feel engineered, used, layered, and physically coherent, but it must remain compact and modular enough for a solo developer to build and iterate.

The three departments should be visually distinct through broad design language:

- **Mine:** rough rock, steel supports, rail lines, pumps, cables, work lights, wet surfaces, and explosive damage.
- **Refinery:** conveyors, crushers, vats, pipes, service platforms, batch containers, warning lights, and controlled spills.
- **Power plant:** larger architectural volumes, reactor containment, coolant systems, turbine machinery, electrical equipment, and emergency infrastructure.

Visual readability remains essential. Department colours, hazard markings, silhouettes, lighting states, machine motion, steam, alarms, and contamination should communicate gameplay without turning the facility into a brightly coloured playset.

The connected site should be understandable from landmarks and circulation routes. It does not need to depict every realistic component of a uranium operation.

## Reactor Hall Visual Target

The reactor hall has a dedicated production specification in [`REACTOR_ROOM_VISUAL_SPEC.md`](REACTOR_ROOM_VISUAL_SPEC.md). Its approved direction is a bright, clean **1990s industrial-science-fiction** room built around a recessed cyan reactor pool and exactly two dominant visible Control Bank A/B drive assemblies. The pool is the hero landmark and a diegetic reactor-state display; the two bank mechanisms provide readable large-scale motion during normal load changes, instability and SCRAM.

This room should remain sterile and maintained rather than rusty or depressing. Use restrained grey/off-white architecture, stainless and muted blue-grey machinery, chunky physical controls, analogue gauges, CRT/early-digital displays, white coolant pipework and limited functional hazard marking. Do not introduce Soviet insignia, steampunk language, a forest of visible control rods, or ultra-modern holographic laboratory styling.

The dimensional and animation constraints in `REACTOR_ROOM_VISUAL_SPEC.md` are authoritative for the reactor art pass and are designed to fit the existing compact Three.js gameplay room rather than enlarge it.

## Characters

Use varied adult body types, ages, faces, ethnic backgrounds, uniforms, and department equipment. Helmets and protective suits should look functional and used, but use simplified shapes and restrained material detail.

Faces may be expressive, but expressions should remain grounded in the game's industrial-comedy tone. Character appeal should come from animation, posture, voice, panic, teamwork, and ragdoll behaviour rather than mascot proportions.

## Concept-Art Standard

Concept art must depict the **shippable visual target**, not an aspirational AAA version that would require a large environment-art team.

Future scenery concepts should show:

- Clearly modular construction.
- Moderate rather than extreme environmental density.
- Simplified low-to-mid poly forms.
- Limited texture complexity.
- Strong atmosphere generated by lighting and weather.
- Adult human scale.
- Clear gameplay routes and interaction spaces.
- A facility that could plausibly be assembled from a reusable kit.

## Prohibited Drift

Future concept art and generated assets must avoid:

- Photorealism.
- AAA cinematic fidelity.
- Photogrammetry-heavy surfaces.
- Excessive micro-detail and greebling.
- Unique bespoke assets for every object.
- Huge open-world industrial vistas as the gameplay standard.
- Dense prop clutter that harms readability.
- Super-deformed anatomy.
- Plush-toy styling.
- Mascot-led branding.
- Juvenile proportions.
- Overly polished mobile-game presentation.

The desired result is **stylised, grounded, readable, atmospheric, modular, and achievable**.
