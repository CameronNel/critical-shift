## Native scene and lighting

The same isolated `AssembledMap.unity` contains all assembled rooms and existing spawn gameplay. Room inventory follows the manifest, so later assembly additions are validated individually rather than rejected by a fixed six-room count. Spawn remains required, along with the other five existing rooms.

Use the pinned Unity 6000.4.3f1 editor with graphics enabled:

```sh
Unity -batchmode -force-glcore -quit -projectPath runtime/unity \
  -executeMethod CriticalShift.Player.Editor.SpawnVisualReview.BakeAndRender \
  -csCapturePath <absolute-spawn-capture-directory> -logFile <absolute-log>
Unity -batchmode -force-glcore -quit -projectPath runtime/unity \
  -executeMethod CriticalShift.Player.Editor.SpawnVisualReview.Render \
  -csRoom mine -csCameraFile <absolute-mine-cameras-json> \
  -csCapturePath <absolute-mine-capture-directory> -logFile <absolute-log>
```

Unity owns its lightmaps, probes and engine lighting. Mine delivery maps contain source shader inputs, including authored shader AO, rather than baked scene illumination. The default path bakes original objects directly. The optional `--batched` path joins only compatible attribute/UV schemas, captures original material coordinates, and hides the original meshes while their temporary replacements bake. This avoids evaluating duplicated geometry and retains per-object data. Materials that depend on Object Info fall back to direct baking. Constant channels use their exact per-material definitions; unused atlas channels skip scene evaluation. Future completed full atlas passes emit `source-recipe-v2` cache records. Reuse requires matching room/exterior source pins, resolution, texture hashes, the exact generated Blender snapshot, Blender version, batching mode and hashes of the exporter, UV, volume and join tools. The tool rejects changes during export. Context-only refresh cannot certify inherited core atlases as a new full cache. Blender persistent data bounds repeated scene compilation. The assembly's dusk lighting and display transform are read from Blender. The player retains spawn's display transform inside its collision bounds and uses the assembly transform outside them.

Blender's surface UVs and geometry stay untouched. The Blender exporter generates a dedicated per-mesh lightmap UV channel with padding, and a Unity import postprocessor checks its range and coverage. Unity's dense-mesh unwrap was superseded after a 38-minute import; the dedicated export avoids that repeated cost. Larger surfaces receive the normal lightmap budget; props smaller than 0.5 m use baked probes and are excluded from lightmap contribution. Split mine flame/bulb surfaces also use probes: their authored point lights provide illumination, rather than forcing tiny emissive faces into a lightmap. Native validation rejects omitted lightmapped surfaces and incorrect emitter contribution flags. The selected depot overlays participate in the same lighting bake; other front-end preview renderers are muted during the bake and restored afterwards. A native bake returning without lightmaps is rejected, including Unity failures that incorrectly return success. For the bake only, static collision instances are released to bound memory and then restored from their unchanged manifest assets before captures or scene saving.

Join validation exports nine ray expectations from Blender geometry. The centre rail is occupied by the authored ore cart at 0.6 m; both side walking lanes are clear at 0.6, 1.7 and 3.0 m. Unity must match the source contacts and all six clear walking rays. The initial all-clear centreline assumption failed and was corrected after tracing the actual cart; no collider faces or map objects were removed. Each room's display transform has its own Unity material, so the mine cannot overwrite spawn's colour profile.

## Timber detail correction

The owner rejected the smeared shed timber. Its boards received approximately 24 atlas pixels per metre: bevel charts and packing left the boards only a few pixels wide. Increasing or softening the lights could not recover the lost grain. The corrected exporter preserves the original packed 1024-pixel wood diffuse and tangent-normal images and their authored UVs in mesh UV2. Repeating images retain their source scale, repeat wrapping and anisotropic filtering. No Blender source image or room is rewritten.

For this source material family, colour is affine in the authored clamped greyscale grain signal. The exporter validates that graph, bakes its two spatial colour coefficients at grain values zero and one, and Unity reconstructs the colour using the original image. The source clamp reaches two, so reconstruction must allow extrapolation above one. Weathering, paint, attributes and source shader AO remain in the coefficient maps. Those two maps use 16 samples to reduce stochastic AO blotches. Normal detail uses the original UV tangent frame and the source normal/bump parameters. Unsupported nonlinear graphs fail export rather than silently using this adapter.

An old flattened atlas is never accepted as a coefficient map. Cache reuse for these maps requires `woodHigh` and the matching `woodRecipe`. Prepared-scene and native material checks also verify the coefficient image, both original images and their bindings. Small Blender/Unity material-only previews show retained grain, but they do not establish final mine lighting or independent art acceptance. Full-room preparation, lighting, matched views and current Player validation remain required.

The rejected final9 wood correction began before that stronger cache schema was introduced. It reused known unchanged legacy atlases and freshly exported wood coefficients; that historical run is recorded as legacy reuse, rather than retroactively certified under `source-recipe-v2`. The final10 corrective iteration completed a fresh rebuild of all nine core/context atlas groups without reuse. Its `source-recipe-v2` record, 70 delivered files, preserved sources and room/collision meshes were independently revalidated before native lighting; records are in `fresh-export-final10/`. Final exported UVs, all delivered texture hashes, geometry preservation and current native evidence remain required. The initial screen-gradient bump approximation failed a controlled native comparison: differentiating the sampled grain produced pixel speckle. The corrected adapter carries Blender's positive, finite `Filter Width` and re-evaluates height at its finite offsets while retaining the original texture gradients. Four source-derived perturbed normals are averaged within each pixel footprint to reduce shading alias that geometry MSAA cannot resolve; this added sampling cost has not been profiled on representative hardware. Controlled normal-only and bump-only references isolate this calculation; final native comparison remains required.

## Fog colour-space correction

A native GPU probe into a linear floating-point render target confirmed that `Material.SetColor` does not decode gamma values for undeclared raw shader uniforms.
